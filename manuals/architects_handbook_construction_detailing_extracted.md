# Architect's Handbook of Construction Detailing: extracted notes

**Original PDF:** `manuals/Architects Handbook of Construction Detailing.pdf` (gitignored, not in the repo). If the file is missing, download it from https://archive.org/details/architectshandbo0000ball_a4w2 (borrow) or purchase or view the publisher page at https://www.wiley.com/en-us/architect's-handbook-of-construction-detailing-2nd-edition-p-9780470381915, and save it under that name.

**Source:** *Architect's Handbook of Construction Detailing*, second edition, by David Kent Ballast, FAIA, CSI. John Wiley & Sons, Hoboken, New Jersey, 2009. ISBN 978-0-470-38191-5. 457 pages as paginated in the PDF (480 pages as listed by the publisher, including covers and blanks).

**Scope of source:** a general architectural detailing reference organized as 178 short, independent detail sheets (numbered like 4-7, each 2 to 4 pages) grouped into seven material chapters that follow the CSI MasterFormat six-digit numbering: concrete, masonry, metal, wood, thermal and moisture protection, doors and windows, and finishes. Each detail sheet gives one drawn assembly plus a description, limitations of use, detailing considerations, coordination required, likely failure points, and material and installation notes keyed to the drawing. The book does not give span tables, structural sizing, nailing schedules, or load calculations; every sheet that touches structural capacity says to verify sizes, spans, and connector counts with a structural engineer. It does not walk through a full building sequence; each sheet is a standalone condition (a wall base, a parapet, a stair landing) meant to be pulled individually into a project's drawings. Coverage of wood construction is narrow: platform and multistory light-frame wall sections, structural insulated panels, glue-laminated beam and column connections, and shop-built cabinetry and paneling (18 sheets total), plus scattered wood-relevant sheets in other chapters (brick veneer over wood studs, wood shingles and shakes at eaves, a wood door and a wood window, wood strip and parquet flooring). There is no dedicated wood roof truss, timber connector catalog, or heavy-timber joinery chapter.

**Most useful for:** platform-frame wall and floor sections at the foundation and roof, hold-down and shear-transfer detailing for multistory wood frame construction, glulam beam-to-column and beam-to-purlin connections, structural insulated panel (SIP) wall-to-foundation and wall-to-roof junctions, generic stair layout geometry (riser, tread, handrail, and guard dimensions usable for a wood stair), wood shingle and shake eave and ice-dam detailing, and wood door, wood window, and wood strip flooring assemblies. For concrete, masonry, and steel work this book gives envelope and connection details rather than structural design.

## Chapter 1, Concrete details (one paragraph)

## Rules for modelling

This source is a reference and design guide, not a build sequence, so the following are transcription rules for pulling a specific detail into a CraftBot wood model, grouped by topic. Every numeric rule below is stated in the source; where the source defers to "verify with a structural engineer" or "verify with the manufacturer," treat the number given as a common default, not a span or capacity rating.

1. Platform frame wall stack (Secs. 4-1 to 4-3): stud and joist spacing 406 or 610 mm (16 or 24 in.) o.c.; sill plate and stud minimum 38 x 89 mm (2 x 4 in.); anchor bolts minimum 13 mm (1/2 in.) dia. at 1829 mm (6 ft) max. spacing, two per sill piece minimum, one within 305 mm (12 in.) of each end; foundation wall exposed minimum 203 mm (8 in.) above grade; floor sheathing 32/16 span-rated plywood 13 mm (1/2 in.) at 406 mm (16 in.) o.c. joists; roof sheathing 24/0 span-rated plywood 10 mm (3/8 in.) at the same spacings.
2. Multistory hold-down chain (Secs. 4-4 to 4-6): model a continuous vertical load path, hold-down connector or continuous rod at the foundation, matching connector at each floor line on the same side of the top plate as the roof connector, terminating with a strap tie into the roof structure. Use a discrete hold-down or strap where overturning is 17,793 to 22,241 N (4000 to 5000 lb); use a continuous tie rod with a shrinkage-compensation device above that, up to over 177,929 N (40,000 lb). Shear sheathing nailed 152 mm (6 in.) o.c. field, 76 mm (3 in.) o.c. edges. Method limited to 3 stories or fewer.
3. SIP walls and roofs (Secs. 4-7, 4-8): model as a solid panel of the nominal thickness (102 or 152 mm/4 or 6 in. wall; 203 to 305 mm/8 to 12 in. floor or roof), not as a stud cavity; sill plate held back 13 mm (1/2 in.) from the panel's outer face for bearing; panel size capped at roughly 2440 x 7315 mm (8 x 24 ft) unless a manufacturer's larger size is specified; treat panel joints as needing a continuous sealant bead.
4. Glulam connections (Secs. 4-9 to 4-13): pick standard widths only from 79, 130, 171, 222, 273, 311 mm (3-1/8, 5-1/8, 6-3/4, 8-3/4, 10-3/4, 12-1/4 in.); depth in multiples of 38 or 19 mm (1-1/2 or 3/4 in.); keep connectors and bolts in the lower half of a beam or purlin cross section and away from member ends and centers; leave minimum 13 mm (1/2 in.) end/side clearance and 76 mm (3 in.) bearing length at a wall bearing; for a purlin saddle, keep the connector within 127 mm (5 in.) of the purlin's bottom edge, and raise unseasoned purlins 0.06 times their depth (seasoned, 0.01 times) above the saddle to allow for shrinkage.
5. Roof decking with exposed glulam (Secs. 4-10, 4-12): use nominal 38 x 140, 64 x 140, or 89 x 140 mm (2 x 6, 3 x 6, 4 x 6 in.) decking with the tongue oriented toward the roof peak; add insulation above the decking rather than in a concealed cavity, with a 152 mm (6 in.) solid blocking course at the low end of the slope if insulation sits above the deck.
6. Stair geometry (Secs. 3-10 to 3-14, applies to a wood stair): tread minimum 279 mm (11 in.), riser maximum 178 mm (7 in.), solved together by 2R + T = 25 (in.); place the vertical work line through the nosing at each transition (first riser, landing nosing, last riser) and align railing, stringer, and stair work points on it; offset the upper flight of a switchback by exactly one tread from the lower flight; set guard height 1067 mm (42 in.), handrail height 864 to 965 mm (34 to 38 in.), handrail extensions one tread at the bottom and 305 mm (12 in.) at the top, baluster clear spacing capped at a 102 mm (4 in.) sphere.
7. Wood shingle or shake eave (Sec. 5-10): minimum 4 in 12 roof slope; spaced sheathing (nominal 25 x 100 mm/1 x 4 in. boards) on centers equal to the shingle exposure, or solid sheathing if an ice shield is required; shingles spaced 6 to 10 mm (1/4 to 3/8 in.) apart; ice shield extends 610 mm (24 in.) inside the exterior wall face in cold climates.
8. Wood door and window openings in a wood frame wall (Secs. 6-5, 6-19): rough opening sized for a 6 mm (1/4 in.) shim gap at each jamb (door and window) and a 13 mm (1/2 in.) shim gap at a window sill for leveling; standard wood door thickness 34.9 or 44.4 mm (1-3/8 or 1-3/4 in.), width 610 to 914 mm (2 ft to 3 ft), height 2032 or 2134 mm (6 ft 8 in. or 7 ft).
9. Wood flooring over a wood or concrete subfloor (Secs. 7-27 to 7-29, 7-31): minimum 19 mm (3/4 in.) expansion gap at every wall line, concealed by the base (51 mm/2 in. minimum for the sprung floor system in Sec. 7-31); strip flooring nominal 19 x 57 mm (3/4 x 2-1/4 in.); subfloor levelness stated in the source as 6 mm in 3 m (1/4 in. in 10 ft); do not model wood flooring directly on a slab without a vapor barrier or sleeper layer.
