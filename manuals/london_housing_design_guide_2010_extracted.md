# London Housing Design Guide (interim edition, 2010): extracted notes

**Original PDF:** `manuals/London Housing Design Guide 2010.pdf` (gitignored, not in the repo). If the file is missing, download it from https://www.london.gov.uk/sites/default/files/interim_london_housing_design_guide.pdf and save it under that name.

**Source:** *London Housing Design Guide, Interim Edition*. Design for London, published by the London Development Agency on behalf of the Mayor of London, August 2010. No ISBN. 108 pages as extracted (form feed count in the PDF, which matches the printed folios throughout). Foreword signed by Boris Johnson, Mayor of London.

**Scope of source:** A planning and space standards guide for new housing in London, not a construction or timber manual. It sets minimum dwelling floor areas by occupancy and storey count, room sizes, ceiling heights, storage, private open space, circulation widths, stair and lift rules, car and cycle parking, daylight and sunlight, and wheelchair accessible housing standards. It also covers urban design principles (density, mix, entrances, shared circulation) and a chapter on managing the design and procurement process. It does not cover structural design, construction detailing, materials specification beyond broad sustainability targets, cost, or nailing and connection schedules. There are no span tables, no framing member sizes, and no construction sequences. Every numbered dimension in this guide is a planning or space standard, not a structural one.

**Most useful for:** Setting plan-layout constraints on a modelled building, minimum room and dwelling floor areas by bedroom and person count, ceiling heights, corridor and stair widths, door clear widths, storage and private open space sizes, and furniture footprints for laying out kitchens, bedrooms and living spaces at the right scale.

## Chapter by chapter notes

### Summary Table of London Housing Design Guide Standards (p11 to 17)

This table lists every numbered standard in the guide in condensed form, each marked Priority 1 (must be met in full) or Priority 2 (strongly recommended, not required). Since every standard also appears in full in its own chapter section, this table is best used as a fast index: scan it to decide which chapter section to open. The full wording of each standard is given in the chapter notes below.

### 1.0 Shaping Good Places (p18 to 25)

General urban design principles, mostly not dimensional. 1.1 Defining Places (standards 1.1.1 to 1.1.2) asks proposals to respond to the physical context, street pattern and public realm, and to avoid blank frontages at ground floor. 1.2 Outdoor Spaces (standards 1.2.1 to 1.2.3) requires compliance with the borough's open space strategy and, for developments with a potential occupancy of ten or more children, play provision under the London Plan SPG. The SPG play space table (p23) sets required play space area by number of children: 100 to 300 sq m for 10 to 29 children, up to 800+ sq m for 80 or more children, with facility types (doorstep, local, neighbourhood, youth space) tied to age band. Communal open space must be overlooked, wheelchair accessible, sunlit and managed (standard 1.2.3).

### 2.0 Housing for a Diverse City (p26 to 31)

2.1 Appropriate Density (standard 2.1.1) ties allowable density to the London Plan density matrix, reproduced on p29: density in habitable rooms per hectare and dwellings per hectare, cross referenced by setting (suburban, urban, central) and public transport accessibility level (PTAL) band 0 to 1, 2 to 3, 4 to 6. Suburban low PTAL ranges from 150 to 200 hr/ha (35 to 55 u/ha at 3.8 to 4.6 habitable rooms per unit); central high PTAL reaches 650 to 1100 hr/ha (140 to 405 u/ha depending on room-per-unit ratio). Central areas are defined as very dense, four to six storeys, within 800 m of a major town centre; urban as terraced houses and mansion blocks, two to four storeys; suburban as detached and semi-detached, two to three storeys. 2.2 Residential Mix (standard 2.2.1) requires the mix of dwelling sizes and tenures to meet borough targets, encouraging maisonettes and family flats with private outdoor space, and a "tenure blind" design approach (defined in appendix 4 as making different tenures indistinguishable in design quality and appearance).

### 3.0 From Street to Front Door (p32 to 43)

3.1 Entrance and Approach. Standards 3.1.1 to 3.1.4: main entrances must be visible from the public realm; the approach from accessible parking to the entrance should be level or gently sloping; entrances illuminated with level access over the threshold; clear door opening 800 or 825 mm depending on approach direction, with a 300 mm nib on the pull side, weather protection and a level external landing (these reference Lifetime Homes Criteria 2 to 4).

3.2 Shared Circulation Within Buildings. This section carries the most geometry in the chapter. Standard 3.2.1: no more than 8 dwellings accessed from a single core per floor. Standard 3.2.2: an access core serving 4 or more dwellings needs an entry phone and electronic lock release system; additional audio-visual security is required if the core serves more than 25 dwellings, more than 100 bed spaces, or more than 8 dwellings per floor, unless a full time concierge with CCTV is provided (in which case more dwellings per core can be accepted). Standard 3.2.3: internal corridors need natural light and ventilation. Standard 3.2.4: minimum width for communal paths, corridors and decks is 1200 mm, with 1500 mm preferred, especially for double loaded corridors or wheelchair accessible dwellings. Standard 3.2.5: where dwellings are entered from communal circulation at the first, second or third floor and no lift is provided, space must be reserved for a future wheelchair accessible lift. Standard 3.2.6: dwellings entered at the fourth floor (fifth storey) and above need at least one wheelchair accessible lift (desirable from the third floor / fourth storey); the seventh floor (eighth storey) and above needs at least two lifts, because walking up more than three flights daily, or six flights occasionally, is considered unacceptable. Standard 3.2.7: every wheelchair accessible dwelling above ground floor needs at least one accessible lift, desirably two. Standard 3.2.8: principal access stairs need "easy access" (defined at footnote 16: risers not exceeding 170 mm, goings not less than 250 mm, minimum width 900 mm measured 450 mm above the pitch line) regardless of whether a lift is provided. The prose adds: lift car minimum internal size 1100 x 1400 mm (an "eight person lift"), landing clear space at least 1500 x 1500 mm square; larger lifts of 1300 x 1400 mm or more are desirable to fit bulky furniture, bicycles, stretchers and coffins.

3.3 Car Parking. Standard 3.3.1: parking provision follows London Plan policy; where PTAL is low, guideline ranges are 1.5 to 2 spaces for 4+ bedroom dwellings, 1 to 1.5 for 3 bedroom, less than 1 for 1 to 2 bedroom. Standard 3.3.2: each wheelchair accessible dwelling gets a 2400 mm wide parking space with a 1200 mm clear access way to one side. Standard 3.3.3: parking siting must not compromise open space quality. Standard 3.3.4: at least one on-plot space capable of widening to 3300 mm; communal bays need at least one 3300 mm space per block entrance or core, in addition to designated wheelchair spaces. Prose notes achievable surface parking density: about 1.0 space per dwelling at 50 dph, 0.5 space per dwelling at 150 dph, before undercroft or underground parking becomes necessary.

3.4 Cycle Storage. Standard 3.4.1: 1 cycle space per 1 to 2 bedroom dwelling, 2 spaces per 3+ bedroom dwelling, for houses and flats alike. Standard 3.4.2: cycle storage must be secure, sheltered, adequately lit, with convenient street access; if provided inside the home it must be in addition to the minimum GIA and storage requirements, not inside habitable rooms or on balconies. Prose gives a guideline floor area of 750 x 2000 mm per bicycle.

3.5 Refuse, Post and Deliveries. Standard 3.5.1: refuse and recycling containers must be accessible to all residents including wheelchair users, on a hard level surface, sited to limit noise and smell nuisance, with cleaning provision. Standard 3.5.2: storage facilities must meet Code for Sustainable Homes and local authority requirements. Not dimensional beyond this; prose covers mailbox placement and delivery logistics.

### 4.0 Dwelling Space Standards (p44 to 61)

This is the chapter with the most load-bearing numbers for a modelling agent.

4.1 Internal Floor Area. Standard 4.1.1 gives the essential minimum gross internal area (GIA) table, reproduced here in full since it is the single most important table in the guide.

| Single storey | 2b3p | 61 |
| Single storey | 2b4p | 70 |
| Single storey | 3b4p | 74 |
| Single storey | 3b5p | 86 |
| Single storey | 3b6p | 95 |
| Single storey | 4b5p | 90 |
| Single storey | 4b6p | 99 |
| Two storey | 2b4p | 83 |
| Two storey | 3b4p | 87 |

| Two storey | 4b5p | 100 |

For dwellings designed for more than 6 people, add at least 10 sq m gross internal area per additional person. GIA is measured to the internal face of perimeter walls, RICS Code of Measuring Practice, and excludes private amenity space, external projections, communal areas, ceilings under 1.5 m, porches, balconies, voids, non-habitable basements and garages (full list in appendix 4). Standard 4.1.2: dwelling plans must demonstrate they accommodate the furniture and activity space listed in appendix 2. General principle: 1 bedroom homes suit 1 to 2 people, 2 bed suits 3 to 4, 3 bed suits 4 to 6, 4 bed suits 5 to 8, 5 bed suits 6 to 10. A single person one bedroom dwelling has a recommended minimum of 37 sq m with a shower room or 39 sq m with a bathroom, though this falls below the formal minimum in table 4.1.1 for a 1b2p unit (50 sq m), since it targets a lower declared occupancy.

4.2 Flexibility and Adaptability. Standard 4.2.1: dwelling plans must allow an alternative seating arrangement in living rooms and accommodate double or twin beds in at least one double bedroom. Not dimensional beyond this.

4.3 Circulation in the Home. Standard 4.3.1: minimum hallway and circulation width 900 mm, reducing to 750 mm at pinch points (e.g. beside radiators) where doorway widths meet the paired specification: 750 mm clear opening needs 1200 mm approach width when not head on; 775 mm needs 1050 mm; 900 mm needs 900 mm (head on approach). Where the hallway is at least 900 mm wide and the door approach is head on, a 750 mm clear opening door is acceptable. Standard 4.3.2: dwellings of more than one storey must allow for a future stair lift, and a suitable space for a through-the-floor lift from the entrance level to a storey with a main bedroom and accessible bathroom; a 1000 x 1500 mm through-floor aperture or soft spot is needed in solid floors, ideally approached from one of the shorter sides and clear of services.

4.4 Living, Dining and Kitchen Areas. Standard 4.4.1 gives combined floor area for living, dining and kitchen space by designed occupancy: 23 sq m (2 person), 25 (3 person), 27 (4 person), 29 (5 person), 31 (6 person). Standard 4.4.2: minimum width of the main sitting area is 2.8 m in dwellings for 2 to 3 people, 3.2 m for 4 or more. Standard 4.4.3: dwellings with 3+ bedrooms need two living spaces (e.g. a living room and a kitchen-dining room), both with external windows; if a kitchen adjoins the living room the partition should be non-load-bearing to allow conversion to open plan; studies do not count as a second living space. Standard 4.4.4: space to turn a wheelchair in dining areas and living rooms, basic wheelchair circulation elsewhere. Standard 4.4.5: a living room, living space or kitchen-dining room must be at entrance level. Standard 4.4.6: windows in the principal living space should start 800 mm above finished floor level (plus or minus 50 mm), with at least one easily operable opening light.

4.5 Bedrooms. Standard 4.5.1: minimum area 8 sq m for a single bedroom, 12 sq m for a double or twin. Standard 4.5.2: minimum width of double and twin bedrooms 2.75 m for most of the room length, to let a wheelchair user pass the foot of the bed when the head is against the side wall. Standard 4.5.3: in homes of two or more storeys with no permanent bedroom at entrance level, there must be space at entrance level usable as a temporary bed space. Standard 4.5.4: the structure above a main bedroom and an accessible bathroom must be capable of supporting a ceiling hoist, with a reasonable route between the two rooms.

4.6 Bathrooms and WCs. Standard 4.6.1: dwellings designed for 5 or more people need a minimum of one bathroom with WC plus one additional WC. Standard 4.6.2: where there is no accessible bathroom at entrance level, provide a wheelchair accessible WC with potential for a future shower there, including floor drainage; a clear shower floor area of 1000 x 1000 mm. Standard 4.6.3: an accessible bathroom must be on the same storey as a main bedroom. Standard 4.6.4: bathroom and WC walls must be able to take adaptations such as grab rails, with support available across a 300 to 1800 mm height band on all walls. Detailed WC approach zone dimensions (p54): WC centre line 400 to 500 mm from a side wall, clear approach zone 350 mm from the WC centre line on one side and 1000 mm on the other, extending 1100 mm in front and 500 mm behind the front edge.

4.7 Storage and Utility Spaces. Standard 4.7.1: built-in general storage, free of hot water cylinders and other obstructions, minimum internal height 2 m, minimum area 1.5 sq m for a 2 person dwelling, plus 0.5 sq m per additional occupant, in addition to storage from furniture in habitable rooms.

4.8 Study and Work. Standard 4.8.1: adequate space and services to work from home (desk, chair, filing cabinet plus circulation). Standard 4.8.2: service controls (switches, sockets) within a height band of 450 to 1200 mm from the floor, at least 300 mm from any internal room corner.

4.9 Wheelchair User Dwellings. Standard 4.9.1: 10% of new housing designed to be wheelchair accessible or easily adaptable, per the GLA Best Practice Guide (summarised in appendix 3).

4.10 Private Open Space. Standard 4.10.1: minimum 5 sq m private outdoor space for 1 to 2 person dwellings, plus 1 sq m per additional occupant. Standard 4.10.2: level access from the home, upstand not exceeding 15 mm. Standard 4.10.3: minimum depth and width of all balconies and other private external spaces is 1500 mm. Prose: up to 5% of dwellings in a development may substitute additional internal living area (equal to the private open space requirement) where site conditions make private open space impossible everywhere; drying line allowance in the furniture schedule is 4 m for 1 to 2 bedroom dwellings, 6 m for 3+ bedroom.

### 5.0 Home as a Place of Retreat (p62 to 69)

5.1 Privacy. Standard 5.1.1: habitable rooms need adequate privacy relative to neighbouring property, the street and other public spaces. Prose notes the old planning yardstick of 18 to 21 m between facing windows as still useful but not to be applied rigidly.

5.2 Dual Aspect. Standard 5.2.1: avoid single aspect dwellings that are north facing, exposed to noise exposure category C or D, or have three or more bedrooms. Standard 5.2.2: where single aspect dwellings are proposed, demonstrate adequate ventilation, daylight and privacy to every habitable room and the kitchen.

5.3 Noise. Standard 5.3.1: layout of adjacent dwellings and location of lifts and circulation should limit noise transmission to sound sensitive rooms. Prose recommends stacking bedrooms next to and above one another rather than above living rooms, and designing to exceed Building Regulations Part E.

5.4 Floor to Ceiling Heights. Standard 5.4.1: minimum floor to ceiling height in habitable rooms 2.5 m, 2.6 m desirable, taller ceilings encouraged at ground floor. Rooms with sloping ceilings under pitched roofs must reach the minimum height over at least 60% of the room's floor area.

5.5 Daylight and Sunlight. Standard 5.5.1: glazing to all habitable rooms not less than 20% of internal floor area (stricter than the Code for Sustainable Homes credit threshold of 2% average daylight factor in kitchens, 1.5% in living/dining rooms and studies, mentioned in the prose). Standard 5.5.2: all homes must provide direct sunlight to at least one habitable room for part of the day; living areas and kitchen-dining spaces should preferably receive it.

### 6.0 Climate Change Mitigation and Adaptation (p70 to 79)

Mostly performance targets rather than geometry, included here briefly since the modelling agent may need to know a building must accommodate green roofs, solar shading, and SUDS features.

6.1 Environmental Performance: minimum Code for Sustainable Homes Level 4 (standard 6.1.1); London Plan sustainable design and construction compliance (6.1.2). 6.2 Energy and CO2: London Plan energy hierarchy (lean, clean, green); CO2 reduction targets 44% (2010 to 2013), 55% (2013 to 2016), zero carbon (2016 to 2031), all versus 2006 Building Regulations (standard 6.2.1). 6.3 Overheating: standard 6.3.1 requires avoiding summer overheating without energy intensive mechanical cooling, favouring dual aspect layouts, shading and deciduous tree planting. 6.4 Water: standard 6.4.1, maximum 105 litres per person per day; standard 6.4.2, flood resilient design per PPS25 where relevant; standard 6.4.3, surface water run-off standards per the Code for Sustainable Homes; standard 6.4.4, Sustainable Urban Drainage Systems and green roofs where appropriate. 6.5 Materials: standards 6.5.1 and 6.5.2 require Code Level 4 compliance on embodied impact and sourcing, at least three of five building envelope elements (roof, external walls, internal walls, upper floors, windows) rated A+ to D on the Green Guide to Specification. 6.6 Ecology: standard 6.6.1, avoid ecologically valuable land, enhance ecological capital per GLA biodiversity guidance. 6.7 Other Considerations: pollution, health and wellbeing, and management criteria in the Code for Sustainable Homes, cross-referenced to other chapters (waste, Lifetime Homes, daylight, noise) rather than restated.

### 7.0 Managing the Design Process (p80 to 89)

Entirely process guidance, no numbered standards and no geometry. 7.1 The Client Role: the client's responsibilities in briefing, team selection, and decision sequencing. 7.2 Design Team Selection: choosing consultants on design ability and value, appointing a design champion. 7.3 The Design Process: a flow diagram (p84) mapping RIBA Design Stages (Inception through Post Practical Completion) against client design management stages and planning stages, including the outline/full planning application and appeal process. 7.4 The Design Process from Inception to Planning: eight key stages from Planning Report through Design Development to Full Planning (RIBA Stage D), each described in a table (p86 to 87), plus advice on contractor procurement (p88) and forward planning for management and maintenance of shared areas (p88 to 89), with a short reference list (p89).

### Appendix 3, Wheelchair Accessible Housing Design Standards (p98 to 103)

A 14 point checklist implementing the GLA Best Practice Guide, itself based on Habinteg Housing Association's Wheelchair Housing Design Guide (WHDG). Each point gives a dimension and a WHDG cross reference for further detail. Key figures, in the order presented:

1. Moving Around Outside: footpaths 1200 mm minimum width; gradients up to 1 in 12 over 2000 mm or 1 in 15 over 5000 mm, with 1200 mm intermediate landings; cross falls not exceeding 1 in 50; flush, shallow-gradient crossings.
2. Using Outdoor Spaces: private garden gates with 850 mm clear opening, operable from both sides by a wheelchair user; level access to balconies clear of door swings.
3. Approaching the Home: covered parking space for every wheelchair user dwelling with a direct external entrance, minimum clear dimensions 3600 mm wide x 5400 mm long x 2200 mm high, slip resistant and nominally level; entrance landing 1500 x 1500 mm minimum (1800 x 1800 mm preferred), 1200 mm depth clear of door swing; entrance canopy at least 1200 x 1500 mm, maximum height 2300 mm; lift internal dimensions per BS 8300 (1100 wide x 1400 deep).
4. Negotiating the Entrance Door: door clear width at least 800 mm (900 mm or wider recommended for communal doors); approach space beside the leading edge of minimum 200 mm (door opening away) or 300 mm (door opening towards the user), extending 1800 mm from the door face; threshold upstand not exceeding 15 mm.
5. Entering and Leaving: turning space of 1800 x 1500 mm behind the closed door, with 300 mm clear beside the leading edge; transfer space of 1100 x 1700 mm to store and charge a second wheelchair.
6. Negotiating the Secondary Door: external landing 1500 x 1500 mm with 1200 mm clear of the door swing; door clear width 800 mm; weather tight threshold.
7. Moving Around Inside, Storing Things: straight passages at least 900 mm wide clear of obstructions; head-on approach to doors needs 200 mm (push side) and 300 mm (pull side) beside the latch edge; 90 degree turns need 1200 mm in one direction and 900 mm in the other (or 900 mm each way with a 300 mm splay); 180 degree turns need 1500 mm clear; doors at an angle to each other need at least 400 mm door-to-corner; sliding doors need 300 mm beyond the doorway on the latch side.
8. Moving Between Levels: through-the-floor lift needs a soft pocket or structural opening of adequate size, clearly identified beneath the floor covering, with circulation space at each level to manoeuvre and call the lift.
9. Using Living Spaces: room layout must let a wheelchair user approach furniture, circulate, transfer to seating, and reach doors, windows, equipment and controls.
10. Using the Kitchen: clear manoeuvring space not less than 1800 x 1500 mm; hob, sink and appliances on the same accessible run; clear knee space retained under sink, hob or oven for future adaptation.
11. Using the Bathroom: fully accessible bathroom with WC, basin and installed level-access shower, with provision for a bath if needed; shower area 1200 x 1200 mm recommended (WHDG minimum 1000 x 1000 mm); second WC in dwellings of 4+ persons, handed opposite to the main WC for left and right handed transfer.
12. Using Bedrooms: access to both sides of double beds, and the outer side of single beds; provision for a full height knock-out panel or door between main bedroom and bathroom, and structural strengthening for a future ceiling hoist track.
13. Internal Doors: construction must permit later fixing of large pull handles or other adaptive fittings.
14. Windows: independent wheelchair-height approach and operation of controls; avoid full-width transoms (horizontal glazing bars) between 800 and 1500 mm high, which would obstruct a seated view out.

## Rules for modelling

Numbered rules a modelling agent can apply directly to a plan layout, each carrying the source standard number or page for traceability.

1. Set overall dwelling floor area (GIA) from the standard 4.1.1 table by bedroom count, person count and storey count (p48). Never model a dwelling smaller than the matching row; add 10 sq m per person beyond 6.
2. Give every habitable room a floor to ceiling height of at least 2.5 m, and prefer 2.6 m; if a room sits under a pitched roof, at least 60% of its floor area must clear the minimum height (5.4.1, p67).
3. Size single bedrooms at least 8 sq m; size double or twin bedrooms at least 12 sq m and at least 2.75 m wide along most of the room's length (4.5.1 to 4.5.2, p53).
4. Size the combined living, dining and kitchen space from the standard 4.4.1 table (23 to 31 sq m by occupancy, p51); make the main sitting area at least 2.8 m wide (2 to 3 person dwellings) or 3.2 m wide (4+ person, 4.4.2, p52).
5. In dwellings of 3+ bedrooms, model two living spaces (a living room and a separate kitchen-dining room), each with an external window (4.4.3, p51).
6. Put a living room, living space or kitchen-dining room at entrance level in every dwelling (4.4.5, p52).
7. Set principal living room window sills 800 to 850 mm above floor level (4.4.6, p52).
8. Give internal hallways and circulation a minimum width of 900 mm, narrowing to 750 mm only at pinch points, and pair door clear opening width with approach width per the table at 4.3.1 (p50): 750/1200, 775/1050, 900/900 (head on).
9. In multi-storey dwellings, reserve a 1000 x 1500 mm aperture or soft spot for a future through-floor lift between the entrance level and the level with the main bedroom and accessible bathroom (4.3.2, p50).
10. Model at least one built-in storage cupboard per dwelling: 1.5 sq m for 2 person occupancy plus 0.5 sq m per additional occupant, minimum 2 m internal height, kept clear of hot water cylinders and other services (4.7.1, p56).
11. Give every dwelling private open space (garden, terrace, balcony or winter garden): 5 sq m minimum for 1 to 2 person occupancy plus 1 sq m per additional occupant, with every balcony or private external space at least 1500 mm deep and wide (4.10.1 and 4.10.3, p59 to 60).
12. Model one bathroom with WC per dwelling; add a second WC where designed occupancy is 5 or more people (4.6.1, p55). Put the accessible bathroom on the same storey as a main bedroom (4.6.3, p55).
13. In a shared-access building, do not route more than 8 dwellings through one core per floor (3.2.1, p37). Give communal corridors, paths and decks at least 1200 mm width, 1500 mm preferred (3.2.4, p37).
14. Provide a wheelchair accessible lift for any core where dwellings are entered at the fourth floor (fifth storey) or above; provide two lifts from the seventh floor (eighth storey) (3.2.6, p38). Size lift cars at least 1100 x 1400 mm internally with a 1500 x 1500 mm landing (p37 to 38).
15. Give principal access stairs "easy access" geometry: risers not exceeding 170 mm, goings not less than 250 mm, stair width at least 900 mm measured 450 mm above the pitch line (3.2.8 and footnote 16, p36 and p38). For a typical 3 m floor to floor height, this works out to about 15 risers at roughly 200 mm rise and 230 mm going, matching the worked example in appendix 1 (p93).
16. Give the main entrance a clear door opening of 800 or 825 mm depending on approach direction, with a 300 mm nib on the pull side and a level threshold (3.1.4, p34).
17. Size car parking bays to London Plan ratios by bedroom count (3.3.1, p40); make at least one bay per plot, or one per communal core, widenable to 3300 mm (3.3.4, p40); give wheelchair user bays 2400 mm width plus a 1200 mm clear access way (3.3.2, p40).
18. Provide cycle storage at 1 space per 1 to 2 bedroom dwelling or 2 spaces per 3+ bedroom dwelling, at 750 x 2000 mm floor area per bicycle if modelled explicitly (3.4.1, p41).
19. When laying out furniture inside a modelled room, use the appendix 2 dimensions directly: double bed 2000 x 1500 with a 400 mm clearance strip along the accessible side(s); double wardrobe 600 x 1200; single wardrobe 600 x 600; three seat settee 850 x 1850; dining table sized 800 wide by 800 (1 to 2p) up to 1650 mm long (7p) (p94 to 97).
20. Where a dwelling is one of the 10% designated wheelchair accessible units, apply the appendix 3 checklist: 900 mm minimum straight passage width, 1200 x 900 mm (or 900 mm splayed) 90 degree turn, 1500 mm 180 degree turn, 1800 x 1500 mm kitchen manoeuvring space, 1000 x 1000 mm minimum shower area, and keep window transoms clear of the 800 to 1500 mm seated sightline band (appendix 3, p98 to 103).
21. Treat the appendix 1 space standards study (p92 to 93) as the reference room-by-room floor plan set: it gives dimensioned kitchen, dining, living, bedroom, bathroom, storage and outdoor amenity footprints for every occupancy from 1 person to 4 bed 6 person, all consistent with the standard 4.1.1 GIA totals, and is the fastest way to block out a plausible floor plan before refining room by room.
22. Do not add structural member sizes, wall thicknesses, or construction sequencing from this source; it specifies none. Use it only for room, dwelling and circulation dimensions, and pair it with a construction manual for how the walls, floors and roof are actually built.
