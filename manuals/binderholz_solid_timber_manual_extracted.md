# Binderholz Solid Timber Manual: extracted notes

**Original PDF:** `manuals/Binderholz - Solid Timber Manual.pdf` (gitignored, not in the repo). If the file is missing, download it from https://www.binderholz.com/fileadmin/user_upload/pdf/products/solid-timber-manual.pdf and save it under that name.

**Source:** Solid Timber Manual 2.0, Binderholz GmbH and Saint-Gobain Rigips Austria GesmbH, overall front matter 3rd edition, February 2023 (the building physics, exterior wall, interior and partition wall, roof and ceiling chapters carry their own separate revision dates, from 1st edition May 2019 to 2nd edition March 2022, and were not all updated together). No ISBN given. 248 pages as paginated in the PDF.

**Scope of source:** This is Binderholz's combined product and systems catalogue for its CLT BBS panel (cross laminated timber, called Brettsperrholz in German) used together with Saint-Gobain Rigips dry lining systems. Around a third of the book is marketing and building-physics background: the case for timber construction, sustainability and forestry figures, and the theory behind sound insulation, heat insulation and fire protection. The remaining two thirds is a catalogue of 139 individually tested exterior wall, interior and partition wall, roof and ceiling assemblies. Every assembly is given as one datasheet: an axonometric cutaway, a labelled section, a full layer-by-layer material list with thickness, heat conductivity, density and flammability class, and a rating box for fire resistance, sound insulation, heat insulation (U-value) and an ecology score. It does not give CLT BBS panel production dimensions (maximum panel length, width, ply count options or transport limits), does not give span tables or a general structural design method (only the single design load line printed on each datasheet), does not give nailing or fastener schedules, and does not describe a build or erection sequence. The only connection or joint details shown are a handful of fire-stop and fire-protection figures for service penetrations and a wall-to-ceiling junction, not a general connections chapter.

**Most useful for:** picking a tested, dimensioned layer build-up (exact millimetre thicknesses of cladding, battens, insulation, CLT BBS leaf and lining) for a CLT BBS exterior wall, interior wall, partition wall, pitched or flat roof, or floor and ceiling slab, and reading off its fire resistance, U-value and sound insulation rating; secondary use for CLT BBS material constants (density, conductivity, burn-off rate) and for the few fire-stop and wall-to-ceiling connection figures.

## 1. Introduction

Marketing and orientation chapter, no geometry to model from. States the manual's purpose (reference work for architects, planners, builders and investors), introduces the two partners (Binderholz for CLT BBS, Saint-Gobain Rigips Austria for dry lining) and lists the testing institutes behind every rating printed on the assembly datasheets: IBS and MFPA Leipzig for fire resistance, ift Rosenheim and Holzforschung Austria for sound insulation, IBO (Austrian Institute for Construction and Ecology) for heat insulation and ecology calculations. CLT BBS received European technical approval ETA-06/0009 in 2006. All assemblies in the manual are also published, with downloadable CAD drawing files, at the Solid Timber Manual Online Tool, massivholzhandbuch.com/en (p. 9).

One figure matters for floor-area planning. Because CLT BBS walls can be built thinner than a masonry equivalent, the wall footprint is about 20% of total floor area in timber construction, against a larger share in conventional construction. That gives roughly 10% more usable floor space at the same exterior building footprint (Figure 4 versus Figure 5, p. 16: 100 m2 conventional flat compared with 110 m2 timber flat at the same outer dimensions).

## 2. Sustainability

Marketing and ecology chapter, no geometry. Covers the case for wood as a carbon store and renewable material, Binderholz's zero-waste manufacturing principle (the whole log is used: sawn timber, CLT BBS, biomass energy, biofuels, no waste stream), Rigips's gypsum sourcing and recycling, and each company's environmental product declarations (EPD) used to calculate the ecology score (delta OI3) shown on every assembly datasheet later in the book. No member sizes, spans or layer build-ups appear here. Worth knowing only so a modelling agent does not go looking for structural or dimensional data in this chapter.

## 3. Building physics

### Sound insulation (pp. 54 to 75)

Short paragraph: covers air-borne sound insulation (Rw), structure-borne and footfall sound, and the flanking or secondary sound paths through junctions between separating and adjoining components (the Kij joint insulation value). Explains that facing formwork with a decoupling installation level, or a double CLT BBS leaf with cavity insulation, improves flanking performance over a single monolithic CLT BBS wall or ceiling, and that a CLT BBS ceiling left in visual surface quality transmits more flanking sound than one with an added floating floor or suspended ceiling. All of the Rw and Ln,w figures quoted in the exterior wall, interior wall, roof and ceiling catalogue chapters are the outputs of the calculation method set out here; the method itself (formulas, correction tables) is not needed to model geometry and is skipped in these notes.

### Heat insulation and humidity regulation (pp. 77 to 79)

Short paragraph: CLT BBS is diffusion-open (steam diffusion resistance factor mu around 40 to 70, see quick reference), so assemblies in this manual are generally designed without a separate vapour barrier or vapour retarder, relying on the CLT BBS panel itself plus the specified insulation and sheathing membrane. The CLT BBS material constants used throughout the catalogue (density, conductivity, specific heat, diffusion resistance, airtightness, flammability class) are tabulated on p. 79 and repeated in the quick reference above.

### Fire protection (pp. 80 to 86)

Fuller subsection, since this is the only chapter with genuine connection geometry.

Fire resistance classes follow EN 13501-2: R (carrying capacity), E (room partition or integrity), I (heat insulation), combined into ratings such as REI 30, REI 60, REI 90 (p. 80 to 81, Figure 17). Every assembly datasheet in the catalogue chapters carries one of these combined ratings plus the boundary conditions (unsupported length and design load) it was tested under.

Burn-off behaviour of unprotected CLT BBS: standard (Eurocode) burn-off rate for pinewood is 0.65 mm/min, but the first 25 mm of each new board layer after a glued joint burns through at an accelerated rate of 1.30 mm/min (p. 82) because the glue line lets a small carbon chip break away. binderholz's own fire testing gives safe-side average burn-off rates of 0.75 mm/min for wall components (3, 5, or more than 5 layers) and 0.90 mm/min for ceiling components, each valid for 30 to 90 minute fire durations (p. 83).

Burn-off of CLT BBS protected by gypsum board planking (Rigips RF fire protection board, Rigidur H gypsum fibre board, or Riduro wooden building slab) is calculated from two times: tch, when carbonisation starts behind the planking, and tf, when the planking itself fails and falls away. Formulas (p. 83), with d = the outer layer's plate thickness in mm (plus 80% of each inner layer's thickness if multi-layered):

- t100 (time to 100 K overtemperature behind the planking) = 2.8 * d minus 22.7, same formula for wall and ceiling, hardwood direct planking or facing formwork/suspended ceiling.
- tch (start of burn-off behind the planking) = 2.8 * d minus 14, same formula in all four cases.
- tf, wall with hardwood direct planking = 2.2 * d + 4; wall with facing formwork in solid timber construction = 1.73 * d + 30.7.
- tf, ceiling with hardwood direct planking = 2.2 * d + 4; suspended ceiling in solid timber construction = 2.43 * d + 0.22.

Fire stops in timber construction (p. 84): developed with Holzforschung Austria for service penetrations (water pipes, ducts, electrical wiring) through solid timber walls and ceilings. Figure 23 gives a fully dimensioned section through a fire-rated pipe penetration: from the fire side outward, a steel pipe with foil-laminated insulation, a fire protection collar (length group AV60), a sealing system, a mounting plate, then (unfired side) 2 x 15 mm gypsum cardboard to EN 520, 6 x 90 mm chipboard screws without washers, and 90 mm CLT BBS; the ring gap around the collar is 0 to 5 mm filled with fire protection joint compound, the overall annular gap is 0 to 10 mm, and at least 30 mm clearance is kept on the side facing away from the fire.

Fire protection evaluation of component joints (p. 85): the flat components were fire-tested, and so were the actual wall-to-ceiling and gypsum-to-CLT-BBS butt joints. A force-fit connection with fastener spacing up to 500 mm prevents burn-through of the joint for 60 minutes; the joint itself is sealed with common retail acrylic or an intumescent product, or, where an elastomer bearing is used between timber elements, the bearing is protected on both sides by mineral wool strips (no separate sealing needed if the elastomer gap is filled with stone wool or fire protection mass). Figure 24 (p. 85) shows binderholz's own processing guideline drawing for an exterior wall-to-ceiling connection detail; Figure 25 shows the elastomer bearing test setup before a fire test. Tests at Technical University Munich confirmed 60-minute smoke-tightness and burn-through prevention for these joints.

## 4. Exterior wall

Every exterior wall datasheet (pp. 96 to 146) has the same anatomy: an axonometric cutaway plus a labelled section drawing (layers lettered A, B, C, ... from inside to outside), a "building physical and ecological rating" box (fire protection REI plus the maximum unsupported length and design load the rating was tested at, heat insulation U-value, sound insulation Rw, ecology score delta OI3), a full layer table (thickness in mm, material, heat conductivity, gross density, flammability class), and an "ecological rating in detail" box (PENRT, GWP100, AP). Designations follow the pattern AW-number-letter, e.g. AW02 b; the number groups a family of related build-ups and the letter distinguishes variants (rear-ventilated, visual surface quality, with or without an installation level, render clad, brick clad).

## 6. Roof

## Rules for modelling

This is a reference and product catalogue, not a build sequence, so these are lookup rules rather than an ordered procedure.

1. Pick the assembly family first: exterior wall (AW), interior or partition wall (IW), roof, steep or flat (DA), or ceiling (DE). Use the contents table for that family (pp. 95, 151, 183, 215, reproduced in full above) to shortlist candidates by target fire resistance (REI), sound insulation (Rw or Ln,w) or U-value, then open that assembly's own datasheet page for the full layer stack.
2. Model every assembly as a flat, constant-thickness layer stack (planar panel with parallel faces), lettered from one reference face to the other exactly as printed on the datasheet (exterior wall and interior wall: inside to outside; roof: inside to outside; ceiling: top to bottom). There are no tapers, cambers or sloped layers in any datasheet examined.
3. The load-bearing layer in every assembly is a single CLT BBS panel, 90 to 150 mm thick, 3-layered up to about 100 mm or 5-layered above that. Do not add a separate structural frame; CLT BBS is the only structural layer, everything else (cladding, insulation, battens, boards) is non-structural buildup.
4. For a rear-ventilated exterior wall or steep roof, always insert an air-ventilation gap: battens at 40 to 60 mm depth on the cladding side, spacing (centre to centre) e = 625 mm, over counter battens of 22 to 60 mm, before reaching the insulation and CLT BBS layers.
5. For a partition wall needing high sound insulation, use two independent CLT BBS leaves (each 80 to 110 mm, see the how-to-clt companion notes for comparable ranges) separated by an air space of about 10 mm plus a mineral wool layer of 50 to 90 mm; do not model a partition wall as a single thick monolithic panel.
6. For a suspended ceiling, add a service void below the CLT BBS slab: a U-suspending bracket layer around 90 to 95 mm (vibration-decoupled) carrying a CD profile, then 60 to 95 mm mineral wool, then a 12.5 to 15 mm gypsum fire protection board.
7. Where a service pipe or duct penetrates a fire-rated CLT BBS wall, model the penetration with a 0 to 10 mm annular clearance around the pipe or collar, at least 30 mm clearance to any combustible layer on the side away from the fire, and treat the ring gap (0 to 5 mm) as filled with a fire protection joint compound rather than left open.
8. At a wall-to-ceiling junction, keep the CLT BBS panels butted (not overlapped or mitred); any fire-rated version of this joint uses a force-fit connection with fasteners no more than 500 mm apart and a sealed or elastomer-bearing joint line, per Figure 24 and Figure 25 (p. 85).
9. Do not invent a panel's maximum length, width or transport size from this source. If an experiment needs those numbers, use a source that states them (the how-to-clt companion notes, or the CLT BBS manufacturer's separate technical datasheet) instead of this manual.
10. Do not invent a construction or erection sequence from this source. It is a static reference catalogue; if an experiment needs an erection order, source it elsewhere.
