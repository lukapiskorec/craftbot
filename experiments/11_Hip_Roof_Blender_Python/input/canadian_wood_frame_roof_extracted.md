## Roof slope conventions (p. 118–119)

- Slope = rise-to-run ratio, **rise always first**. Two conventions:
  - **Imperial:** run always 12 (framing square). 45° = 12/12; 4/12 = 4 in. rise per 12 in. run.
  - **Ratio:** for slopes < 45° the *first* number (vertical) is 1 (e.g. 1:5 = 1 vertical per 5 horizontal); for slopes > 45° the *second* number (horizontal) is 1 (e.g. 5:1). Avoid mixed units (1 mm in 10 m).
  - 4/12 ≡ 1:3. Angular expressions acceptable where high accuracy needed.
- Pitched roofs vary from **1:6 to 1:1 or more**. Minimum slope for asphalt shingles is 1:6; different installation rules for ≥ 1:3 and < 1:3.
- Roof must have water-shedding surface, air and vapour barriers, thermal insulation and ventilation. Most Canadian houses: prefabricated trusses + unheated ventilated attic. Cathedral ceilings and flat roofs still need a vented space above insulation.
- Roof joist/rafter sizes by species/grade/live load: Tables 29–32 (pp. 295–301, not in excerpt).

## Chapter 11 — Ceiling and Roof Framing

### Pitched roofs (p. 119)
- Prefabricated trusses most often used; site-framed rafters sometimes. **Gable roof is simplest**; hip or L-shape roofs can also be trussed (**Fig. 77**: L-shape trussed roof — plan with hatched roof planes and a valley; isometric shows ridge, valley trusses, roof sheathing, gable end, girder truss, common trusses, double wall plates).

### Prefabricated roof trusses (pp. 119–122)
- Advantages: designed for local snow loads, quality control, less lumber waste, faster enclosure; provide surface for sheathing and ceiling, space for insulation.
- **Raised heel trusses** (**Fig. 78**) used in most new houses — deeper space at the eaves for insulation; truss anchors as requested by manufacturer/code. Energy codes lead to vertical depth ≥ 300 mm (12 in.) at outer face of exterior wall for trusses, rafters and roof joists.
- Trusses typically span exterior wall to exterior wall, no intermediate bearing → partitions placed freely; continuous air barrier/vapour retarder applied to underside before partitions.
- **Fig. 79 — Types of prefabricated roof trusses:** king-post · Howe · Fink or W · mono-pitch · parallel chord · scissor truss · mansard.
- Storage flat and clean; spans < 6 m (20 ft) installed by hand, longer need lifting equipment. Lift vertically to avoid lateral bending.
- Erection: gable truss first, braced to ground and wall; each additional truss generally **600 mm (24 in.) o.c.**, toe-nailed to top plates, temporarily braced (**Fig. 80** — gable ends normally sheathed before end braces). Then permanent bracing (**Fig. 81**): (a) top chord plane, (b) lateral web bracing, (c) web/bottom chord plane. Plan view: lateral braces across top chords, diagonal brace nailed to web repeated at ~6 m (20 ft); end elevation: 38 × 89 mm web bracing; side elevation: sheathing, blocking, lateral brace, diagonal braced bay at both ends and ~6 m intervals. Sheathing adds stiffness. Do not cut or alter trusses.

### Site assembly of pitched roofs (pp. 122–127)

**Fig. 82 — Types of pitched roofs:** Flat · Monopitch · Gable · Hip · Gambrel · Mansard.

- Gable roof: rafters/trusses same length and pattern. Dormers add light/headroom (Fig. 88). **Hip roof: common rafters fastened to a ridge board, hip rafters supporting the jack rafters (Fig. 83B).**
- Liveable attics need insulation, air barrier, vapour barrier; framing may need to be upsized for insulation depth.
- **Ceiling joists** support ceiling finish and act as ties between exterior walls / opposing rafters; may carry loads from dwarf (knee) walls → size accordingly (Table 33); if they carry floor loads use floor joist tables (Tables 19, 20).
- Nail ceiling joists **after wall framing, before rafters** (rafter thrust otherwise pushes walls out). Joists tie lower ends of rafters in roofs sloping **≥ 1:3**; nail ceiling joist to side of each pair of rafters (**Fig. 83**).
- **Hip roofs:** where jack rafters run perpendicular to ceiling joists, a **stub joist** ties the jack rafter back to the ceiling joist (**Fig. 85**). Lap and nail joists or splice at centre bearing wall for a continuous tie. Nail counts depend on slope, spacing, snow load, house width (Table 24).

**Fig. 83 — Roof framing and attachment**
- **A Gable roof:** ridge board, rafter, collar brace, collar tie, gable end stud, ceiling joist, top wall plate.
- **B Hip roof:** hip rafter **at least 50 mm (2 in.) deeper than jack rafters**, jack rafter, ceiling joist, top plate. Isometric: four hip rafters rising from the corners to a short ridge (shown as a vertical post marker at the apex in the drawing), jack rafters from plates to hips, common rafters from plates to ridge, ceiling joists spanning the short direction.

- Dwarf walls perpendicular to ceiling joists (**Fig. 84**) add roof load; increase joist depth one size when slope > 1:4; when ≤ 1:4 use roof joist tables.
- **Fig. 84 — Rafter heel supported on a rafter plate:** rafter plate nailed to top of each ceiling joist with ≥ two 100 mm (4 in.) nails; rafter; ridge beam; exterior wall; knee wall with top and bottom plates and stud in line with each ceiling joist and rafter; struts at 1.2 m (4 ft) o.c.; ceiling joists lapped directly above interior load-bearing wall.
- Hip rafters ~51 mm (2 in.) deeper than common/jack rafters reduce space along end walls; in low-slope roofs the outside ceiling joist may not fit at normal spacing → **doubled joists** positioned to suit, then **tail joists** toenailed to outside wall plate and end-nailed to the doubled joist, at the same spacing as main joists (**Fig. 85**: jack rafter, hip rafter ≥ 50 mm deeper, ceiling joist, double ceiling joist, normal location for ceiling joist if space available, stub joist, end wall framing top plate).
- **Cutting rafters:** proper angle at ridge; **bird's mouth** notches at eaves; heel bears directly over exterior wall. Rafters placed: (a) directly on wall plates (Fig. 83); (b) on a rafter plate on the ceiling joists (Fig. 84); (c) on a load-bearing wall supported on the exterior wall plate (**Fig. 86**, used where part of the outside wall is inset — ceiling joists extend beyond exterior wall and are nailed to rafter sides).
- **Ridge board or ridge beam** ensures a level ridgeline. Erect rafters in pairs, nail to ridge, toe-nail lower ends to wall plate. Pairs directly opposite or **offset at ridge by ≤ their thickness** (offset needed when joists are lapped at the centre wall).
- **Slope < 1:3 → vertical support at peak:** 38 × 140 mm (2 × 6) ridge beam supported at 1.2 m (4 ft) by 38 × 89 mm (2 × 4) struts, or a load-bearing wall. Ridge support eliminates outward thrust → continuous ties unnecessary. Ridge beams also needed on steep roofs where rafter ends can't be tied.
- **Intermediate support** between ridge and exterior walls reduces rafter span (span measured from intermediate point to ridge/eave):
  - Slope ≥ 1:3: 38 × 89 mm **collar ties** nailed to each rafter pair; ties > 2.4 m (8 ft) braced against buckling with a 19 × 89 mm continuous member at right angles near centre (three 76 mm nails each end).
  - Slope < 1:3: **dwarf bearing wall** (Fig. 84), single top plate allowed if rafters over studs.
  - **Struts** 38 × 89 mm nailed to rafter side, bearing on load-bearing partition, **≥ 45° from horizontal** (Fig. 86).
  - Rafters perpendicular to ceiling joists: dwarf wall on a **beam between ceiling joists**, beam underside ≥ 25 mm (1 in.) above ceiling finish via blocks at bearing points.
  - Hip/valley rafters: beam with roof strut; or for a few rafters at a hip end a **"strongback"** of two 38 × 89 mm nailed on edge under the rafters, supported by 38 × 89 mm struts radiating from a common point on the interior bearing wall.
- **Hip and valley rafters ~50 mm (2 in.) deeper than common rafters** → full contact with the angle-cut of jack rafters (Figs. 83, 85, 87). Hip roof: jack rafters nailed to hip rafters and wall plate. Valley: jack rafters nailed to valley rafter and ridge.
- **Fig. 86 — Rafter heel supported on load-bearing wall:** ridge board; intermediate struts ≥ 45° to horizontal; 38 mm (2 in.) splice plate; interior bearing wall; rafters notched and bearing on top plate of exterior wall; continuous nailer and soffit supports; ceiling joists extend beyond exterior wall, nailed to rafters and toe-nailed to double wall plate; studs in load-bearing wall directly under each rafter; wall sheathing; exterior wall. Inset shows a house with an inset wall portion.
- **Fig. 87 — Framing at a valley:** valley rafter, jack rafter, common rafter, top plate, corner wall studs (inside corner of an L-plan: valley rafter rises from the inside corner; jack rafters from each wing's plate to the valley).
- **Dormers (Fig. 88):** rafters each side **doubled** to support side studs and valley rafters; top ends of valley rafters on a **double header** between the doubled rafters (joist hangers). Usual method: install roof sheathing first, frame dormer, cut sheathing flush with framing around opening; **bottom plate on top of the sheathing** supports side studs and is nailing base for wall sheathing. Size window for flashing/detailing.

### Gable-end framing and projections (pp. 127–129)
- After roof framing, nail **gable-end studs**; in unfinished attics wide face may be parallel to wall; cut to rafter angle, toenail to plate and rafter underside with ≥ four 63 mm (2½ in.) nails each end.
- **Projection < 300 mm (12 in.)** (**Fig. 90 — Narrow projection**): **rake rafter** supported by ridge beam, rafter header, blocking and sheathing; 19 mm (¾ in.) nailing strip fastened to rafter above gable wall; blocking 600 mm (24 in.) o.c. toenailed to strip, end-nailed to rake rafter; soffit nailed to these, fascia added; gable wall sheathing taken up to underside of roof sheathing; rough fascia supports heel of rake rafter.
- **Projection > 300 mm (12 in.)** (**Fig. 89 — Wide projection supported by "lookout" rafters**): gable-end studs with narrow face parallel to sheathing + top wall plate; **lookouts** same size as rafters, **600 mm (24 in.) o.c.**, end-nailed to the first rafter inboard and to the gable top plate; blocking between lookouts at the wall line supports sheathing and inner soffit edge; **lookout length ≥ 2 × overhang**; **double rafter** where lookouts project > 1.5 rafter spaces into the roof. Labels: ridge beam, rafter, top plate of gable end wall, blocking between lookouts, lookouts 600 mm o.c., ceiling joist, top plate, header or rough fascia, fascia board at eave and gable end, corner studs.

### Low-slope roofs (pp. 129–130)
- Must be more watertight (poorer drainage); may carry decks/traffic → durable materials.
- Rafters doubling as ceiling joists = **"roof joists"**, sized for roof + ceiling loads (Tables 29, 30); may need wider lumber / engineered wood for insulation and ventilation depth.
- Laid level or nearly level; **slope ≥ 1:50 for drainage** via ledger strip on bearing wall or tapered strip on joists.
- Overhang via **lookout rafters** (**Fig. 91**): if projecting > 1.5 joist spaces, two roof joists nailed together form a header; outside rafter header end-nailed to lookouts and joists (nailing for sheathing, fascia, soffit). Labels: alternative framing layout (dashed), roof joist, lookout rafter, header, exterior wall plate, rafter header.

### Roof space ventilation (pp. 130–131)
- Always ventilate above insulation; membranes are vapour-resistant so vapour must be removed by ventilation.
- **Ice dams:** heat loss + sun melts roof snow, refreezes on cold eaves → water backs up (Fig. 100). Airtightness + insulation + ventilation keeps attic cold.
- Clearance between insulation and sheathing: **≥ 63 mm (2½ in.) without baffles, 25 mm (1 in.) with baffles**.
- **Vent area: ≥ 1/300 of insulated ceiling area for slopes ≥ 1:6; ≥ 1/150 for slopes < 1:6**; roof, gable or eave vents (**Fig. 92** soffit ventilation: baffle, perforated soffit, pre-finished fascia, airflow; **Fig. 93** ridge vent and gable vent). Uniformly distributed, ≥ 25% at bottom, ≥ 25% at top.
- Batt insulation between roof-ceiling joists (low slope): ≥ 63 mm vent space — achievable with **38 × 64 mm (2 × 3) cross-purlins** at right angles over joists (may be shimmed for slope). Rigid insulation above sheathing → no ventilation needed.
- Vents must exclude rain, snow, insects; baffle if moisture source; corrosion-resistant metal/plastic screens.
- **Attic access hatch ≥ 0.32 m² (3.4 sq ft), no dimension < 500 mm (20 in.)** except very small attics.

### Sustainable housing insight — Ch. 11 (p. 135)
- Energy: raised heel trusses / deep rafters for insulation over exterior walls; consider deeper members or closer spacing for future solar panels.
- Resource: trusses use select lumber + metal plates for long spans, less wood; suit local climate; fast; space for insulation/ventilation; all roof shapes; no site cut-offs.
- Affordability: plan attic conversion — stair location, size ceiling joists as floor joists, movable insulation, steeper slopes shed snow and enclose more attic volume, rough-in services.

## Chapter 12 — Roof Sheathing and Coverings

### Roof sheathing (pp. 136–138)
- Sheathing-grade plywood, OSB or lumber planks over trusses/joists/rafters → nailing base for roofing + lateral bracing.
- **Panels (Fig. 97):** face grain **at right angles to framing**, joints offset (staggered), **2–3 mm (⅛ in.) gap**. Nails **150 mm (6 in.) o.c. minimum at edges, 300 mm (12 in.) o.c. intermediate**. Edge support: metal H-clips, 38 × 38 mm (2 × 2) blocking, or tongue-and-groove plywood. Thickness per Table 35 (depends on support spacing and edge support). Stagger fasteners at adjoining panel edges. Staples for 9.5 mm sheathing: 1.6 mm thick, 38.1 mm long, 9.5 mm crown, crown parallel to framing. Built-up roofing used as walking deck needs ≥ 15.5 mm (⅝ in.) sheathing.
- **Lumber boards (Fig. 98):** laid closed (edge to edge), ends supported, joints staggered. High wind/earthquake: laid **diagonally** or engineered. Usually 19 mm (¾ in.) thick; 17 mm allowed when supports ≤ 400 mm o.c. Boards ≤ 184 mm (8 in. nominal) wide: two 51 mm (2 in.) nails per bearing; wider: three; high wind: 63 mm nails. **No boards wider than 286 mm (12 in. nominal).** For wood shingle roofs boards may be **spaced** at the shingle exposure on centre (damp climates; lets shingles dry). Fig. 98 shows spaced method (nailing strips) above and closed method (roof boards, joint) below, on rafters/ceiling joists/plate.
- **Details (Fig. 99):** **50 mm (2 in.) clearance** of sheathing and framing from interior masonry/metal chimneys (12 mm / ½ in. for exterior masonry chimneys); header around opening; nail sheathing to rafters and headers. Fit sheathing tightly at **valleys and hips** and nail to valley/hip rafter → smooth base for flashing. Figure shows L-plan roof with valley, chimney opening with header, rafters, ridge board, ceiling joist, plate, stud.

### Eave protection (pp. 138–140)
- Membrane along roof edge against water entry. **Not required** over unheated garages/porches, slope ≥ 1:1.5, or regions ≤ 3,500 heating degree-days.
- Extends **≥ 900 mm (36 in.) up the roof** (measured along slope) to **≥ 300 mm (12 in.) inside the inner face of the exterior wall** (horizontal). Installed under shingle starter course; usually self-adhering bituminous or roll roofing membrane (type S smooth or type M mineral-surfaced roll roofing, joints lapped ≥ 100 mm and cemented; or peel-and-stick).
- **Fig. 100 — Eave protection:** top: no protection — snow, trapped water backs up through membrane, thin ice slab under snow, melted snow running down underside of sheathing, ice in eavestrough; bottom: protection directs water off the roof. Prevent ice damming by air-sealing ceiling, ceiling/wall junction, penetrations; adequate insulation; eave venting. Keep skylights away from ice-dam areas.

### Roof coverings (pp. 140–146)
- Install as soon as sheathing is on. Asphalt shingles common on pitched roofs; prefinished metal/galvanized steel/aluminum common in some regions (sheds snow); also roll roofing, wood shingles, shakes, sheet metal, concrete/clay tile; built-up with gravel/cap sheet for low slopes. Min/max slopes per covering in Table 36.
- **Minimum slopes: 1:6 asphalt shingles (low-slope application); 1:4 wood shingles; 1:3 hand-split shakes and asphalt shingles (normal application).**

#### Asphalt shingles on slopes ≥ 1:3 (**Fig. 101**)
- Min No. 210 grade; square-butt strip **310 × 915 mm (12 × 36 in.)** or **335 × 1000 mm**, three tabs, exposure **130 mm (5 in.) or 145 mm (5¾ in.)**; 21–26 strips per bundle ≈ 3 m² (32 sq ft). Store flat; distribute bundles on roof.
- Sequence: eave protection → **starter strip** (shingles with tabs removed, or reversed shingles, or type M roll roofing) projecting ≥ 12 mm (½ in.) beyond eaves, rakes and fascia (drip edge; metal drip edges also ok), nailed 300 mm o.c. → first course with butt edge in line with bottom of starter strip → subsequent courses at allowed exposure (depends on slope, shingle type/length), aligned with chalk lines or underlay print lines.
- **Four large-head roofing nails per strip**, penetrating ≥ 12 mm into sheathing. Self-sealing tar seals. Fig. 101 labels: roof sheathing, eave protection, roofing nail, tar seals, exposure, fascia board, starting course.

#### Asphalt shingles on low slopes 1:6 to 1:3
- Three thicknesses everywhere except first two courses, incl. hips and ridges; **exposure ≤ ⅓ shingle height**. Starter strips over a continuous cement band ≥ 200 mm (8 in.) wide; first course cemented with a band exposure + 100 mm (e.g. 250 mm for 150 mm exposure); subsequent courses band exposure + 50 mm (200 mm for 150 mm exposure); band 25–50 mm above butt edge to avoid defacing. Four fasteners above exposed portion. Cold cement ≈ 0.5 L/m², hot ≈ 1 kg/m². Only needed below 1:4 — special low-slope shingles keep three thicknesses at ≥ 1:4.

#### Wood shingles (**Fig. 102**)
- Western red / white cedar heartwood (decay-resistant, low shrinkage), No. 1 and No. 2 grades; width **75–350 mm (3–14 in.)**; other species preservative-treated. Underlay/felt not usually required; eave protection required.
- First course **doubled**, upper overlapping joints, both rows projecting ~**25 mm (1 in.) beyond fascia** (figure: 12 mm / ½ in. for drip). **6 mm (¼ in.) gap** between shingles; joints offset **≥ 40 mm** from joints in course below and not aligned with the two previous courses.
- **Two nails per shingle**, at exposure + 40 mm from butt (e.g. 125 mm exposure → nail at 165 mm), **20 mm edge distance**; hot-dip galvanized/corrosion-resistant. Flat-grain shingles > 200 mm wide may be split and nailed as two.
- Fig. 102 labels: eave protection ≥ 300 mm beyond inside face of wall, roof sheathing, two nails per shingle, 20 mm edge distance, wood shingles, exposure, 40 mm lap, 6 mm space, fascia board, first course (double), project shingles 12 mm for drip.

#### Shakes (**Fig. 103**)
- Cedar hand-split: **length ≥ 450 mm (18 in.), width 100–350 mm (4–13¾ in.), butt 9–32 mm (⅜–1¼ in.)**. Min pitch **1:3**.
- Over spaced or closed sheathing; spaced: 19 × 89 mm (1 × 4) or wider strips on centres = exposure, never > 250 mm (10 in.); closed recommended where wind-driven snow.
- Exposure **190 mm (7½ in.) for 450 mm shakes, 250 mm (10 in.) for 600 mm (24 in.) shakes**.
- 900 mm (36 in.) No. 15 felt at eave line; starter course doubled (tripled for texture), bottom course 380 or 450 mm shakes. After each course, 450 mm (18 in.) No. 15 felt strip over top portion, bottom edge at **2 × exposure above butts** (e.g. 600 mm shakes @ 250 mm → felt 500 mm above butts, covering top 100 mm of shakes and ~350 mm onto sheathing). Gap **6–9 mm**; side joints offset ≥ 40 mm; froe-end (smoother) uppermost.

#### Finish at ridge and hips (**Fig. 104**)
- Asphalt: shingle squares (⅓ strip) over ridge/hip, blind-nailed, lapped to same coverage; orient cap away from prevailing wind. Wood: 150 mm (6 in.) wide shingles alternately lapped and blind-nailed, over No. 15 felt; flashing sometimes under wood ridge.

#### Built-up roofs (**Fig. 105**)
- Specialist installers; ≥ 3 layers of felt each sealed with tar or asphalt; gravel or cap sheet as ballast/UV protection. Coal tar and asphalt products incompatible.
- Eave trimmed with metal edging/flashing; gravel stop or cant strip with flashing. At walls: mopped to cant strip, turned up ≥ 150 mm (6 in.), sheathing paper and siding lapped over (50 mm min lap). Masonry-clad wall: membrane turned up, flashing over, counter-flashing embedded ≥ 25 mm in mortar, down ~150 mm, lapping ≥ 100 mm. Single-ply membranes also usable. Fig. 105 labels: siding, sheathing membrane, 50 mm minimum lap, membrane flashing, cant strip, roof sheathing, built-up roofing, metal gravel stop, fascia board.

#### Sheet metal roofing (**Fig. 106**)
- Widths **762–914 mm (30–36 in.)**, any length. Min slope **1:4** unless manufacturer proves lower. Over spaced supports: sheets designed for snow load, support under each end joint. Min thickness: 0.33 mm galvanized steel, 0.46 mm copper/zinc, 0.48 mm aluminum. Fig. 106 accessories: nailing strips for sheet metal roofing, eave starter, ridge cap, end wall flashing, valley, cottage hip, side wall flashing.

#### Concrete and clay tile roofing
- Considerably heavier; roof structure engineered for the additional load.

### Sustainable housing insight — Ch. 12 (p. 147)
- Deflect all water away from roof and walls; drip edges beyond materials below.
- Orient roof for future solar; finish service life ≥ renewable system; stiffer sheathing/closer framing for panels; building-integrated PV tiles.
- Make roof surface dimensions **multiples of 400 or 600 mm (16 or 24 in.)** to reduce panel waste; metal recyclable; wood renewable; keep roofs clear of leaves/branches to dry; concrete tile durable but heavy — extra framing/seismic reinforcement.

## Summary for procedural modelling — hip roof framing sequence

Members and rules for generating a site-framed hip roof (Figs. 82, 83B, 85, 87, 97–99):

1. **Walls:** double top plates on exterior walls; studs/plates define the rectangular footprint. Optional interior load-bearing wall along the centre.
2. **Ceiling joists:** span the short direction at 400 or 600 mm o.c. (305/406/610 mm to suit panels), nailed to plates **before rafters**; lapped over the centre bearing wall. Typical 38 × 140 mm (2 × 6) for ~4.3 m span @ 400 mm. Near hip ends where the hip rafter leaves no room, use a **doubled joist** set inboard plus **stub/tail joists** to the end plate at the same spacing (Fig. 85).
3. **Ridge board:** level, centred on the short span; length = building length − building width (for equal-pitch hips, hip rafters meet the ridge ends at 45° in plan). Slope < 1:3 requires a ridge beam (38 × 140 mm) on 38 × 89 mm struts at 1.2 m, or a bearing wall.
4. **Common rafters:** from plates to ridge board in opposite pairs (or offset ≤ one thickness), bird's-mouth at the plate, heel bearing directly over the wall; depth ≥ 300 mm at wall face if insulation requires; spacing = joist spacing. Typical 38 × 184 mm @ 300 mm or 38 × 235 mm @ 600 mm for a 4.7 m span at 1:3 (Ottawa snow). Ceiling joist nailed to the side of each rafter.
5. **Hip rafters:** four, from each building corner to the ridge ends, at 45° in plan; **depth = common rafter depth + 50 mm (2 in.)**.
6. **Jack rafters:** from plates to hip rafters (hip jacks) on both the long and short sides, spacing continuing the common-rafter module, progressively shorter toward the corner, nailed to hip and plate.
7. **Valleys (L-plans):** valley rafter (also +50 mm deep) from the inside corner to the ridge; valley jacks from ridge to valley.
8. **Intermediate support if needed:** collar ties 38 × 89 mm on rafter pairs (slope ≥ 1:3), struts ≥ 45°, dwarf walls (slope < 1:3), strongback (2 × 38 × 89 mm) under hip-end jacks.
9. **Eaves/overhangs:** rafter tails project past the wall; fascia at rafter ends; soffit with perforated vent; gable-end overhangs (gable roofs only) via rake rafter (< 300 mm) or lookouts 600 mm o.c. (> 300 mm).
10. **Sheathing:** 1220 × 2440 mm panels, long dimension (face grain) perpendicular to rafters, staggered joints, 2–3 mm gaps, H-clips on unsupported edges, nails 150 mm edge / 300 mm field; trimmed tight at hips and valleys. Or 19 mm boards ≤ 286 mm wide, closed (edge to edge, joints staggered over supports) or spaced at the shingle exposure for wood shingles/shakes; diagonal in high wind/seismic areas.
11. **Covering:** eave protection 900 mm up slope; starter course; asphalt shingles (exposure 130/145 mm, ≥ 1:3) or wood shingles (exposure ~125 mm, ≥ 1:4) or shakes (exposure 190/250 mm, ≥ 1:3); ridge and hip caps lapped away from prevailing wind.
12. **Ventilation:** soffit intake + ridge/gable exhaust, ≥ 1/300 of ceiling area, 63 mm (or 25 mm with baffles) clear above insulation at eaves.
