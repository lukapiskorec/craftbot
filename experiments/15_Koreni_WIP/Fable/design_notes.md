# Design notes, experiment 15, Fable run

Decisions in the order taken, each with the version it applies to, the alternatives and why they lost. Rationale section 6 is compiled from this file.

## 2026-09-09, before v01: reading the site

**Contour interval.** Decision: 0.1 m intermediates, 0.5 m index lines, 9.4 m total fall over 96 m. Method: pixels sampled along the plot axis of `site_02.png` (road-end centre 520,1125 to far-end centre 945,255 in the 1502 x 1466 image; a pixel counts as blue when b > r + 50 and b > g + 20, orange when r > b + 60, g > b + 10, r > 150; consecutive same-colour runs count once): 74 blue and 19 orange crossings; two parallel lines a third of the width either side gave 72/18 and 69/21. Alternative rejected: 0.5 m intermediates, which would give 46 m of fall and a 48 percent average slope; the section sketches 07 to 09 at their own scale (16.6 px/m from the 96.4 m boundary spacing, box heights 85 px = 5.1 m) show 8.4 to 10.8 m. The survey count and the sketch scale agree only under the 0.1 m reading.

**Profile shape.** Decision: three straight segments, 5 percent to x = 26, 8 percent to x = 50, 14 percent beyond. From the index crossings: 11.0, 9.2, 7.2, 6.3, 5.8, 5.3 m per 0.5 m over the first 50 m (4.5 to 9.4 percent), then 3.7 to 4.1 m per 0.5 m (12 to 14 percent). Alternatives: a single 10 percent plane (loses the visible steepening that makes the far end a cliff edge, and puts the bridge's undercroft 1.5 m too low at the pavilion); the sketch's own profile (gentle to x = 66, steep after) which the survey contradicts. The survey wins because the brief says to model the actual terrain. Concave profile chosen deliberately so that the ground solid, if ever needed, is convex.

**Cross fall.** Decision: none. The contours in the middle third are within about 15 degrees of perpendicular to the axis; the gully near the road and the oblique far-end contours would cost a ruled or faceted surface and make every wall base non-planar. Recorded as an assumption; if a user round asks for it the terrain becomes a `ruled.Ruled` sheet per segment.

**Orientation.** Decision: `site_02` north-up, axis bearing N25E, road uphill at the SSW end. The cadastral plan has no north arrow; Serbian cadastral extracts are drawn north-up as a rule. Consequence: the long faces look ESE and WNW, neither south; no solar argument is made for the open-side pattern, which follows sketch 02 instead.

**Plot as a rectangle.** The plot tapers from 16.8 m at the road to 20.1 m at the far end; modelled 20 m wide. The house never comes within 5 m of a long boundary, so the taper changes nothing in the model.

## 2026-09-09, before v01: reading the sketches

**One plan, three sections.** Decision: all three variations share the pavilion-plus-straight-bar plan (sketch 06) and differ only in section. Alternatives: give each variation one of the sketched plans (03 fork, 04 bend, 05 hook). Rejected because the brief makes the slope strategy the variable and mixing plan and section changes would make the three incomparable; also the fork and bend wings run 20 degrees off the axis and every kit function is axis-aligned, so a rotated wing would cost the Builder a second coordinate frame for a third of each house. The far-end offset bar in `site_01` (the second rectangle shifted 3.5 m to the WNW) is a cousin of the fork and is rejected for the same reason.

**Pavilion offset.** The square at the road end sits against the WNW side of the building zone in sketches 01, 03, 04, 05 while the bar sits on the axis. Kept: pavilion y = 9..15, bar y = 7..13, a 4 m overlap so the entry passes through the pavilion into the bar.

**Bar width 6.0 m.** Sketch 01 reads 3.5 to 4 m (55 px at 15.2 px/m). A 4 m bar leaves 3.6 m clear, too narrow for an open living room with a core against one wall. 6.0 m gives 5.7 m clear and the bedrooms at 12 m2 need only 2.2 m of bar length. Recorded deviation S-05.

**Open-side alternation (sketch 02).** The green edges alternate sides segment by segment. Two readings: green marks the glazed side, or green marks a planted/retaining edge. Adopted the first: it is the only reading that gives a plan rule, and it produces a solid wall on the other side, which the lateral system along x needs anyway (a 60 m glass bar with no solid wall has no shear element along its length). The segment limits in metres come from the green line ends at 15.2 px/m: 660 to 770 px = x 21..28 ESE, 760 to 1010 = 28..44 WNW, 985 to 1340 = 44..66 ESE, the wing 1215 to 1555 = WNW; the first segment extended back to x = 11 to cover the entry bay.

**Ellipse in sketch 06.** Read as the view from the far end downhill (it crosses the bar's end obliquely, aligned with the long axis of the plot's far end). Consequence: the far end of every variation is the terrace; no room turns its back on it.

**Three boxes of the section sketches.** Sketch 07 has three boxes of 5.4, 15.4 and 20.8 m separated by 10 m gaps, the first at the building line, so the first box is the pavilion. Sketch 09 draws the roof continuous over the gaps, so the gaps are low parts of one continuous volume, not open air; the stairs sit in them. With the survey's 14 percent beyond x = 50, a 20.8 m third box at one level would stand 2.5 m above the ground at its far end, so variation C uses four floor levels (pavilion plus three) at 1.2 to 1.35 m steps, each level about 18 m long; three stairs instead of two. Recorded deviation S-08.

**Ridge position.** Sketch 08 puts the ridge at the downhill end of box 2, sketch 09 at its uphill end. 09 is the later drawing (it adds roof thickness and the stairs) and its ridge over the uphill end makes the living hall the tallest room, so 09 wins: ridge at x = 11.

## 2026-09-09, before v01: the three strategies

**A bridge.** Level floor at +0.60 from the pavilion to x = 71. The near abutment is the pavilion's rear wall; without it the first girder bay would have its underside in the ground (z(11) = -0.30, girder underside at floor - 0.20 slab - 0.70 girder = -0.30). Raising the floor to +0.90 was the alternative (six risers in the pavilion); rejected because four risers already read as "stepping up onto the bridge" and the extra 0.3 m lifts the whole roof. The far abutment is a concrete bedroom box at x = 53..65 rather than more steel columns: it gives the house its second level and its stair, and a bridge with two concrete abutments and three intermediate column pairs is the honest span diagram (12, 12, 12, 6, 12 over the box, 6 cantilever). Columns at 12 m rather than 4 m so the undercroft reads as open; girders 700 deep at L/17 (mine) pay for it.

**B cut.** One level at -2.60, the ground level at x = 45.4, chosen so the cut at x = 11 (2.30 m) and the plinth at x = 66 (2.61 m) are the same height: half cut, half fill, the two halves the same length (34 and 21 m of house, but the trench is 10 m wide and the plinth 6 m, so the volumes come out near 400 and 165 m3 plus the courtyard grading). Sunken strips 2.0 m wide along both sides so the glass on the cut half looks at a wall 2 m away and gets light from above, and so the retaining wall is not the facade. Alternative: retaining walls as the facade itself (the house as a buried box with glass only at the downhill end); rejected because then the cut half has no long-side glass and the alternation rule of sketch 02 cannot be read.

**C steps.** Four levels every 18 m with 1.2 to 1.35 m steps, each level cut at its uphill end and standing at its downhill end by up to 1.3 m. Alternative: levels graded so each sits fully on cut ground (no plinths); rejected because the 14 percent lower segment would then need 2.5 m steps and 17 risers per flight. The folded roof follows sketch 09: ridge at the hall's uphill end, 8.2 percent fall to the far end, so every level has a tall uphill end and a 3.3 m downhill end; no parapet, a fascia, drainage to the far end.

**Common family.** Posts SHS 150 at 4 m, roof beams IPE 330 transverse, slabs 200, cores 200 concrete with lids, mullions 60 x 150 at 1.333 m, glass 24 mm. One facade function serves all three; A's floor is the only different floor. Concrete walls 250 (pavilion, box, plinth), 300 (retaining), 200 (cores, closed sides). All labelled mine until the Researcher returns; see `sources.md` for what the manuals changed.

**Terrain as a skin.** A 0.30 m plate under the surface rather than a solid: the overlap check treats every mesh as solid, so a terrain solid would flag every footing; a skin with holes cut around the crossings passes the check and still draws the ground line in every section. Alternative: a terrain solid split into convex blocks around every embedded element; rejected as a version of work per footing.

**Placement.** Strips at y = 0, 30, 60 with 10 m gaps rather than one continuous 60 m wide ground: three separate strips make the "three copies of the same site" reading explicit and keep the section cuts per variation clean.

## 2026-09-09, before v01: the reference images

Skimmed (01, 12, 25, 38) for material language only, per the brief. They show board-marked concrete walls and plinths, a thin steel roof plate on slender round or raking columns, frameless storey-height glass, and a long thin slab as an object in the landscape. The concept already uses that family (concrete boxes and retaining walls, steel posts and beams, glass in a 1.333 m grid). No requirement is derived from them and the phase-2 comparison does not use them. Two things seen and deliberately not adopted: raking or A-frame columns (reference 12) and a corrugated steel roof deck (reference 38): the roof deck is concrete in all three variations so the folded roof of C and the parapets of A and B are one material family, and raking columns would give the bridge of A a second structural idea it does not need.

## 2026-09-09, before v01: the research round (sources.md, concept v2)

The Researcher read AHCD, CMHC and LHDG (the timber manuals proper have nothing for a concrete, steel and glass house and were left closed) and returned 95 rules. Where a manual gave a number it replaced mine; the list of what changed and why:

- Bridge floor +0.60 to +0.90. CMHC's 300 mm crawl-space clearance is the only ground-clearance rule in the manuals; at +0.60 the girders touched the ground at x = 11 and cleared it by 0.3 only from x = 17. The alternative, a 0.3 m cut under the first bay, contradicts the bridge idea. Five risers from the pavilion at +0.15 instead of four from 0.00.
- Pavilion floor 0.00 to +0.15, courtyard and sunken-strip paving 150 below the floors: CMHC's slab top at least 150 above finished grade.
- Storefront, not curtain wall: AHCD 6-6 defines the one-storey glass in an opening as a storefront; mullions 51 x 152 from the catalogue sizes, glass 25 mm IGU, head slip joint 6 mm. Captured glazing rather than structural silicone because AHCD gives captured as the storefront default; a flush glass face was the alternative and would have hidden the mullion rhythm the sketches' tick marks suggest.
- Parapet 400 to 600 above the slab top: AHCD measures the 305 minimum from the roof surface, and the 2 percent drainage wedge puts the surface 270 above the slab at the high edge. The wedge is modelled (one prism per roof) because it is the one roof detail visible in a section; the drain line at the low edge is not.
- Suspended slabs 250 to 300: no manual sizes them; ACI's L/20 for a simply supported one-way slab at 5.5 to 6.0 m, labelled as practice, not a manual clause. A centre spine beam was the alternative and would have added a member family for one slab in B and two in C.
- Pad footings 1.5 to 1.8 square and 0.6 thick with base plates: CMHC Table 4 scaled from 3 m to 12 m column spacing, thickness at least the projection from the base plate.
- Footing undersides 1.2 m below grade: CMHC Table 3's threshold for undefined soils. A local frost depth for Koreni is not in the manuals; I refused an online search since a value between 0.8 and 1.2 m moves nothing visible. Stepped footings in 0.6 m steps (CMHC Fig. 35): A's box footing gets three steps instead of one.
- Guards 1.07 inside (AHCD and CMHC agree above a 1.8 m drop), terrace 1.10 kept; a store of 3 m2 added to core 1 (LHDG 4.7.1); entrance door 0.95 with a 0.30 nib (LHDG 3.1.4); egress windows for A's lower bedrooms (CMHC ch. 15).
- B's retaining walls are outside the manuals: CMHC Table 5 stops at 1.50 m free-standing and 2.30 braced; B needs 2.3 and 2.75. Kept as engineered cantilever L-walls with base 0.6 H (standard proportions, labelled), toe 0.15 H. The alternative, lowering B's cut to 1.5 m, would put its floor at -1.80 and its plinth at 3.4 m, turning the cut house into a raised one; the strategy needs the deep cut. Recorded in the deviation ledger (concept 8).

Refused searches: retaining wall proportions, plate girder span/depth, European section dimensions, local frost depth. Each is a labelled rule of thumb whose exact value changes nothing at model scale, and the manuals rule says external material only where a manual is silent and the number matters.

## 2026-09-10, after v02: two conflicts raised by the Builder

**B's entry stair headroom (R-40).** My error: the landing at +0.15 sits under the low roof's beam underside at +1.03, 0.9 m of headroom; I had compared the roof top with the floor. Decision: keep the Builder's high bay (x = 11..15.28 at the pavilion roof, slab top +3.65) and rewrite R-40 so that the pavilion, the high bay and the low parapet are what shows from the road. Alternatives: a stair inside the pavilion, rejected because a 4.5 m run plus a 1.2 m landing needs 5.7 of the pavilion's 6 m and turns half the garage floor into a stair well, which moves the cut and the end retaining wall from x = 11 to x = 5; an external stair down the WNW courtyard strip, rejected because it puts the only entrance in the open for a 2.9 m descent. The high bay costs nothing structurally (the same transverse frame at a greater height, the closed wall carried up) and gives B a 5.9 m tall stair hall, which the cut house otherwise lacks. Reading from the road changes from "one pavilion" to "pavilion plus its stair bay", still a single low silhouette.

**C's clear height (R-45).** Same error: 3.3 was roof top minus floor without the 0.20 slab; as built L1 3.42, L2 3.30, L3 3.10. Decision: relax R-45 to 3.0 m clear (LHDG 5.4.1 asks 2.5) and leave R-41 and R-44 as built. Alternatives: end R2 at -0.73 (7.85 percent), rejected because it flattens the fall that matches sketch 09 for 0.2 m of air; lower L3 to -4.10, rejected because it deepens the cut at x = 47 to 1.37 m, adds a riser to stair 3 and raises the plinth at x = 66 to 1.11 m, for the same 0.2 m.

## 2026-09-10, after v03: three Builder questions

**B high bay spandrel (R-40).** Dropped; the built full-height storefront stays (head under the high roof's edge beam, slab edge, parapet). The spandrel was my addition when I wrote the high bay from the pavilion's proportions, not a structural need: the edge beam spans the same 4.28 m either way. The storefront in the high bay is 5.7 m tall (floor -2.60 to head about +3.1), beyond the one-storey opening AHCD 6-6 has in mind; the 51 x 152 mullions stay as the family and the height is recorded as plausible only, not sized. A concrete panel replacing the edge beam was the alternative and would have made a second roof-edge detail for one bay.

**Closed-wall doors (R-10).** Confirmed the Builder's rule: a door in a closed wall only where the outside surface is within one riser of the floor. A door must land somewhere; A's closed walls stand in the air over the undercroft, so A has none (its exits are the pavilion and the terrace); B gets two onto the courtyard, C two onto grade. Written into R-10 with the positions.

**Programme check (R-19).** The rooms are zones between the cores in an open plan, so no wall names them. Check split: the script prints each variation's zone list with x ranges and areas (the LHDG minimums are checked there), the Inspector confirms the built elements that bound the zones. Not waived; ticked when both halves pass.

## 2026-09-10, after v04: structural review (rationale 6b), reference set aside

Read from the v04 script, not the concept, so the findings are about what stands.

**Vertical load paths, all sound.** A: roof slab 200 on IPE 330 at 4 m on SHS 150 posts (glazed side) and the closed panel (pockets), posts on the bridge slab over the girder line; bridge slab 200 on IPE 300 floor beams at 4 m between two 700 plate girders; girders in 250 pockets of the x = 11 wall, on 300 cap plates over HEB 240 columns at 23, 35, 47, and continuously on the box long walls over 53..65 (the girder's underside at 0.00 is the wall top), cantilever 6 m with a 12 m back span; columns on 300 base plates on 1.8 x 1.8 x 0.6 pads (about 580 kN per column from 36 m2 of floor and roof at 16 kN/m2, 180 kPa on the pad, plausible for the site's soil, not calculated); box walls on strip footings stepped three times; pavilion box on strips. B: roof on beams on posts and the closed concrete wall; floor on ground over gravel to x = 46, suspended 300 over 46..70 on 250 plinth walls on stepped strips; end wall as one 300 element from the L-base to the pavilion roof, side stems on 1.95 m bases (0.6 H with H = 3.25 from the base underside, the script's own arithmetic). C: folded roof on beams at the plane's height on posts and closed walls that follow the plane; each level on ground or on 300 suspended slabs on plinth walls; step walls at 29 and 47 from 0.5 below the lower floor to the upper floor level, on cross footings; ridge wall at 11 from -1.70 to the roof.

**Bearings.** Roof beams bear 200 mm in the closed-wall pockets (CMHC beam pocket at least 90); girders bear full width on the cap plates and along 12 m of box wall; suspended slabs bear on the full 250 walls; A's transverse beams butt the girder webs, which is a bolted shear connection the brief leaves undrawn (R-55, ticked as a record).

**Lateral, the finding.** Across y no variation had a shear element above the floor except at the uphill end (the pavilion box; A's box at the far end too). The cores stopped at 2.7 + 0.2 under a beam underside at 3.3, so the roof diaphragm leaned on nothing for 50 to 60 m; the roof beam frames are pinned (posts pinned, beams in pockets) and the "portal" I wrote for A's columns does not exist as built: the cap plate sits under the girder and the transverse beam meets the girder web at the top flange, so there is no moment path from beam to column. Decisions: (1) cores full height with beam pockets in all three, concrete in B and C, sheathed steel-stud panels in A (R-51), so each house has four to five cross walls 2.4 m long distributed along its length; (2) A's columns declared pinned both ways and the deck read as a 6 m deep diaphragm spanning 42 m between the two concrete boxes, depth to span 1:7, which is the honest bridge diagram and needs no change (R-52); (3) B's step wall at 15.28 and end wall, C's ridge wall, named as the uphill cross walls. Alternatives rejected: cross walls at the segment switches (28, 44, 60), which would close the glass-to-glass corners the alternation reading depends on; moment-connected post frames, which a 150 SHS at 3.3 m could carry for wind on a house but which the model cannot show as different from pinned; K-bracing in the glazed bays, which the storefront grid would hide and the sketches never suggest.

**Other findings.** B's and C's plinths are hollow undercrofts as built, better than the filled boxes of version 1 (no earth pressure on their inside faces); they need access openings (R-53, 0.6 x 0.9, my number: the Researcher did not extract CMHC's crawl-space access size). The pavilion roof slab at 200 spans 5.5 m between the y = 9 and 15 walls, breaking the L/20 rule that gave B and C their 300 suspended slabs; changed to 300 with the top unchanged (R-54). Recorded as not modelled: movement joints in C's 59 m roof prism and B's 55 m parapets, drainage behind the retaining walls, anchor bolts and end plates, rebar, the 5 percent grade away from walls. Sections are plausible for their spans, not calculated.
