# Construction Manual of Prefabricated Timber House — Extracted Notes

**Source:** *Construction Manual of Prefabricated Timber House*, FRIM Technical Information Handbook No. 5, by Mohd Shukari Midon, Chu Yue Pun, Hilmi Md Tahir, Nor Azian Mohd Kasby. Forest Research Institute Malaysia (FRIM) / International Tropical Timber Organization (ITTO), 1996. ISBN 983-9592-55-6. Produced under the project "Research on the Utilization of Tropical Timber in Construction". Extracted from `Construction Manual Of Prefabricated Timber House.pdf`.

**Scope:** Step-by-step construction of a prefabricated 'low-cost' single-storey timber-framed house (platform frame method, **Modular Panel System**) designed for Malaysia/tropical climates (not suitable for very strong winds or hurricanes). Panels may be factory-made or assembled on site DIY-style. Internal partitions are non-load-bearing and can be rearranged without affecting structural strength. Appendix contains 9 sheets of detailed technical drawings.

## Chapter 2 — Fabrication systems (overview)

1. **Pre-cut system** — oldest: timber cut/notched/drilled at factory, marked, bundled and assembled on site. More accurate than site cutting, minimal waste.
2. **Modular panel system** *(adopted in this manual, fig. 1)* — components built in factory by 2–3 men without cranes. Uniform wall panels sized on module M = 1.22 m (4 ft) to tally with Malaysian cladding sheets (plywood, hardboard, chipboard, cement board). Exploded view: floor joists → floor panels → wall panels → ceiling panels → roof truss.
3. **Large size panel system** (fig. 2) — panels up to 10–12 m (a dwelling length) factory-assembled; good for mass building many small houses; fewer site joints but higher transport/handling costs and cranes needed.
4. **Volume element system** (fig. 3) — dwellings completely finished at the factory on foundation beams; buyer sees the finished house beforehand; fastest site erection but needs trailers and cranes.

## Chapter 3 — Design of the house

- Traditional Malaysian construction is **post and beam** (beams 2–4 m apart on posts; non-load-bearing infill panels). The manual's house is instead **timber-framed (wood-framed)** — traditional in North America/Scandinavia, comparatively new in Malaysia.
- Two timber-frame methods:
  - **Balloon frame** (fig. 4): studs run full building height; intermediate floor joists side-fixed to studs. Only for 2–4 storey buildings.
  - **Platform frame** (fig. 5) — *used here*: shell built one storey at a time with storey-height wall panels; each floor becomes the construction platform for the next storey or roof.
- **Wall panel make-up** (fig. 6): top and bottom horizontal plates spaced for the designed height (2745 mm / 9 ft); vertical studs nailed between them at 610 mm centres (the text's "0.16 m centres" is a misprint — 610 mm everywhere else); noggings added to strengthen sheathing edges; sheets fixed one or both sides. All framework butt-jointed and nailed. Panels ≤3000 mm long for man-handling. Panels erected over the prepared platform, roof placed over to complete the building.
- **Advantages of timber-framed construction:** (1) simple transport of pre-assembled panels; (2) less site labour (more saved if factory-assembled); (3) panels erected manually by 2–3 men, no cranes; (4) speed — all wall panels of a house up in 2–3 days; once roofed, plumbing/electrical proceed in the dry; (5) low-cost high thermal insulation; (6) flexible design, modification and extension.

## Chapter 4 — Basic principles

- **Modular dimensioning:** best use of sheet materials + easy later extension. Local sheets are 1220 × 2440 mm, so planning uses multiples of **610 mm**; a 610 mm grid governs plan layout and positioning/fixing of partitions, roofs, joists (fig. 7).
- **Example plan (fig. 7):** proposed house 50 m² (7320 mm frontage × 5490 mm, with kitchen/WC/bath wing 4270 wide × 2440 deep at rear); future extension 3050 mm (bedroom 3) brings it to 63 m². Rooms: BR1, BR2, Living/Dining, Verandah, Kitchen, Bath, WC.
- **Load path (fig. 8):** unlike post-and-beam, the *wall panels are load-bearing*. Roof trusses (@1220 crs), studs (@610 crs) and floor joists (@610 crs) are kept **vertically in line** for efficient load transfer roof → studs → joists → ground. Section shows: roof truss / head binder / top plate / vertical stud / nogging / lintel / cripple stud / door / bottom plate / base plate / floor board / floor joist / bearer.
- **Load-bearing vs non-load-bearing walls (fig. 9):** load-bearing walls support floor/roof above. Non-load-bearing walls are partitions only — made **shorter** than load-bearing walls in the same storey so roof/floor joists above do not rest on them (clearance at top).
- **Openings:** doors/windows need a **lintel** spanning the opening, ends carried on **cripple studs** each side (fig. 8). Small openings need no lintel — simply framed between studs.
- **Panel-making tolerances:**
  1. Stud depth uniform throughout so finished panel-with-sheathing width is constant (e.g. for 47 × 97 studs, the 97 mm dimension must be accurate/regularised).
  2. 1–3 mm vertical gap between plywood sheathings for expansion, covered with beading.
  3. Finished length of each prefabricated panel 1–3 mm less than the drawing size.
- **Detached or linked houses:** houses may be linked by a **separating wall** (fig. 10; grid 305 mm, min 305 mm separating wall zone); empty space between neighbouring houses acts as a firebreak; minimum spacing per Uniform Building By-laws 1984.

### Sequence of construction (summary from Ch. 4)
- Main building at front on a raised platform **700 mm above ground**; kitchen at the back on ground-level concrete. Floor-to-ceiling **2745 mm (9 ft)**. Perspective in fig. 11.
- Order: concrete footings → posts on footings → bearers bolted across posts → floor joists with stiffening cross-pieces on bearers → T&G floor boards conceal-nailed → platform ready. Then pre-assembled wall panels erected over platform (kitchen, on ground level and not connected to the platform, can proceed simultaneously) → head binders/wall plates bind panel tops and support trusses → panels fixed sideways with nailed iron plates/angles, concealed with beading → prefabricated trussed rafter roofs on wall plates → rafters braced diagonally, purlins nailed → roofing sheets (now weathertight) → doors and windows → plumbing, wiring, sanitation → paint → occupy.

## Chapter 5 — General requirements and specification of materials

### Timber
- **Strength Group B** per Malaysian Standard M.S. 544:1978 (SIRIM).
- **Grade:** Standard structural grade per Malaysian Grading Rules (MGR) Part III Section J.
- **Sizes:** as drawings. Exposed surfaces planed; unexposed sawn unless regularised (e.g. the 97 mm of 47 × 97 studs must be fixed/accurate).
- **Moisture content at installation:** floor boards ≤ 18%; all other timber ≤ 22%.
- **Preservative treatment:** pressure impregnated with CCA to dry salt retention **5.6 kg/m³**, CCA penetration ≥ **12 mm**.

### Panels/boards
- **Plywood:** from a reputable mill; **WBP** (water-boil-proof) or **MR** (moisture-resistant) type per position in the plan.
- **Cement-bonded particleboard** (cemboard) for kitchen; fix per manufacturer's instructions.
- All other materials per good construction practice.

## Chapter 6 — Details of construction

### Site access
- Check/construct vehicle access, especially in remote areas. Electric power (mains or generator) and water (mains, well, river, or transported) still needed on site. Allocate storage space for prefabricated components away from the building area.

### Preparation of site
- Examine soil (timber house is light → most normal soils fine). If termite-infested, apply **anti-termite soil treatment before construction**.
- Clear vegetation, remove roots/cellulosic material during grading; ensure good drainage. Frontal (platform) part may sit on level ground or a gentle slope — level the platform by adjusting **footing level or post length** (fig. 12). Floor ≥ **700 mm above ground** for ventilation. Kitchen area at rear levelled by cut/fill with proper ramming.

### Setting out
- Don't build too close to the boundary (circulation; gap to neighbours acts as firebreak; min spacing per Uniform Building By-laws 1984). Locate near one end of a large plot to allow future extension (fig. 13).
- Drive temporary pegs at the four platform corners → set out with **batter boards**: 2 per corner (8 total), each of two vertical stakes **50 × 100 mm × ~1 m** driven ≥1 m beyond footing lines, with a horizontal board **25 × 150 mm** nailed on. All board tops levelled — surveyor's level or **transparent water-filled hose** (fig. 15); highest corner is the datum.
- Strings stretched between boards; positions marked with a nail or saw kerf so strings can be replaced. Two adjoining sides set with steel tapes; squareness checked with the diagonal — sides 7230 and 5400 mm → diagonal **√(7230² + 5400²) = 9096 mm** — or by the **3-4-5 rule** (fig. 16, plumb bob to a nail in a stake at the first corner). Fix three corners, establish the fourth, check both diagonals.
- Intermediate footing points marked with steel tape and plumb bob, always measuring from corners along the string. **All 12 footing positions set** (fig. 14: grid 3 × 2440 by 2 × 2700 centre lines of footings).

### Footings
- Remove strings, keep batter boards for later alignment/levelling.
- Excavate 12 holes **600 × 600 mm, 400 mm deep**; ram hardcore to **150 mm thick**; blind with a level of sand.
- Formwork for concrete; footing depth min **300 mm** (more on sloping contours if all footing tops are kept level; alternatively vary post lengths so footing tops need not be level). Footing top ≥ **50 mm above ground**.
- Before pouring: **12 mm MS bar reinforcement near the bottom**, **MS U-straps for posts set at the top**, aligned/levelled from batter-board strings and plumb bobs (fig. 12(i)).
- Concrete mix **1 : 2–3 : 4** (cement : sand : stone; sand per wetness), preferably machine-mixed (thorough hand mixing acceptable); pour and rod/vibrate to a dense concrete without disturbing reinforcement.

### Platform
- **Posts:** MS bearing plate **120 × 120 × 6 mm** placed inside the U-strap; posts **120 × 120 × 499 mm** dropped in; each fixed to the U-strap with **2 bolts** through predrilled holes (fig. 17).
- **Bearers:** one on each side of the post tops (**2/60 × 194**, i.e. paired), **4 bolts** through both bearers and post; washers under bolt heads/nuts bearing on timber. Lengthened with **splice pieces 2/25 × 194 × 300** between posts. Keep posts truly vertical and all bearer tops level and horizontal.
- **Joists:** 47 × 145 @ 610 crs on bearers, **slant-nailed with 75 mm nails**, one each side (fig. 18). Stiffening cross-pieces against tilting: **50 × 50 at each end**, **50 × 75 at the centre**, seated in notches cut in the joist tops (fig. 19).
- **Floor boards:** T&G strip flooring laid across joists (Ch. 6 text: 30 × 145; Table 1/drawings: 22 × 145); fit groove over tongue snugly and **blind-nail through the tongue at 40–50°** into the joist (fig. 20). **Header joist 20 × 194** nailed to joist ends, top level with the floor boards, concealing the joist spaces (fig. 21).
- Platform complete → kitchen construction can start separately or simultaneously.

### Wall panels
- Panels pre-assembled at the factory (more accurate) or on site (pre-cut pieces save labour; assemble on a level surface such as a concrete slab or trestles).
- **Base/sill plates** fixed over the platform first: **47 × 112 (exterior)**, **47 × 84 (interior)** to match panel widths (fig. 22 shows base plate layout with joints). Check positions and corner squareness, then nail to floor boards with **76 mm nails @ 300 mm centres in a zigzag** pattern.
- **Erection (fig. 23):** first panel is corner panel No. 1; skew-nail its bottom to the base plate; hold upright with **temporary diagonal braces** (removed only when panels are permanently fixed). Then subsequent panels. **21 panels over the platform, 6 over the kitchen.**
- Panels **2745 mm high**, longest **2550 mm** → man-handled without cranes. Four **temporary lifting handles** fixed ~1000 mm high near the ends of each double-sheathed panel; removed once in position.
- **Wall plates/head binders** over panel tops: **47 × 112** on thicker exterior walls, **22 × 84** on thinner interior walls (thin 22 mm plates keep the tops of non-load-bearing walls clear of trusses). Nailed with 76 mm nails @ 300 crs zigzag.
- **Panel-to-base-plate fixing:** **Pryda knuckle nail plates 2N10** (89 mm wide × 63 mm deep, 2 rows of 10 punched-out nails) or similar @ **610 mm centres on the exterior side**; interior side skew-nailed with 76 mm nails to the base plate. Metal plates and nails covered by **plywood skirting 9 × 100 mm**.
- **Panel-to-panel (side) fixing:** 3 Pryda 2N10 plates, evenly spaced. **Corner (right-angle) fixing:** 3 **Pryda angle plates 3NA** (44 × 44 × 95) on the interior angle. All plates covered with **plywood beading** (2/9 × 72; fig. 24: length-wise panels and corner panels, nail plates 63 × 89 @ 610 spacing).
- Erect **kitchen walls before roof trusses** — the extended trusses of the main building rest on the rear kitchen wall.

### Kitchen
- Area ~**3.1 × 5.1 m** behind the main building. Excavate to level; hardcore rammed to **100 mm**; blind top with sand and wet liberally; pour **100 mm concrete floor, mix 1:2:4**. **Strip footings 150 wide × 50 thick** additionally under wall-panel lines (fig. 25 drawing labels: conc. curb 50 × 115 for wall panels; conc. floor 100 mm; strip footing 200 × 100; slab 2443 × 4500).
- **Concrete curbs replace timber sole plates** (wet nature of the kitchen): **50 mm thick × 115 mm wide** for thicker walls, **50 × 84** for thinner. **Bolts 12 × 100 mm** cast vertically into the curbs, 38 mm embedded, threads exposed; ~**2 bolts per panel** (fig. 26: anchor bolt @ 1 m crs).
- Kitchen panels sheathed both sides with **cement-bonded particleboard: 10 mm outside, 8 mm inside**. Pre-assembly covers only the 10 mm side; **elongated holes** drilled in the bottom rail to match curb bolts; panel dropped over bolts and tightened with nut and washer; 8 mm inside sheet fixed in situ (predrill nail holes in cemboard).
- Wall plates on top; side/corner attachments with Pryda (or equivalent) plates as for plywood panels.

### Roof trusses
- Trussed rafters preferably factory pre-assembled. Joints: **nailed 9 mm WBP plywood gusset plates both sides** (per plan) or **proprietary punched metal plates** (then fabricated by the licensed factory which also sizes the plates; timber member sizes unchanged). All members **35 × 72 mm**.
- **Erection:** first truss at one end, nailed to wall plate, **temporarily braced to the ground at two places with 50 × 100 timber** (fig. 27) — must be firm, all other trusses fasten back to it. Second truss placed **upside down** with ends on opposite wall plates, peak hanging down, then **tilted up with a long "Y"-ended pole** (fig. 28); nail to wall plate, brace to first truss; repeat. Attachment: **skew-nailed 4/100 mm nails per truss-to-plate joint, two each side** (fig. 29).
- **Gable ends** (platform area): closed with **6 mm plywood** cut to size; roof-space **ventilation through holes drilled in these gable sheets**.
- **Kitchen end:** four trusses have **extended rafters** covering the kitchen, resting on the kitchen wall plate; **two additional 35 × 72 beams between each pair of trusses** stiffen the kitchen roof.
- Manual erection by **4 workers** (one on each wall nailing, one at truss centre fixing the top, one general/lifting); crane faster if available.
- **Permanent bracing** against wind sway: horizontal braces at right angles to trusses; **diagonal braces at 45°** from top of the gable-end truss to the bottom of the fourth truss (fig. 30; braces 22 × 97). Then remove temporary bracing.
- **Purlins 35 × 72** on top of trusses, skew-nailed to each rafter with **two 76 mm nails**, one per side (fig. 31). Fascia and barge boards fixed to rafter/purlin ends.
- **Roofing:** corrugated **asbestos-free fibre cement sheets** laid over purlins per manufacturer's instructions. Building now dry regardless of weather.

### Doors and windows
- Openings pre-formed in wall panels. Door openings keep a **full-length bottom rail** during handling (prevents twisting); after erection the exposed rail portion is **cut out together with the base plate** at the opening (fig. 32).
- Doors/windows factory-assembled, delivered for installation. **Door sizes: 700 (WC/bath), 770 (kitchen/rooms), 840 (main entrance); all 2100 mm high** from floor level. Door/window dimensions must match panel openings exactly. **All doors open inwards.**
- **Flush doors:** framework assembled square, plywood glued and nailed both sides.
- **Windows:** frames made on site in the panel openings. **Adjustable aluminium louvre frames with 8 leaves** fixed to the wooden window frame; glass sheets cut to size slide into the louvre slots; glass sheets also used as **fixed glass louvres** above the adjustable ones.

### Railings and stairs
- **Railings:** rails 47 × 97, vertical balustrades 20 × 20; rail top at **1000 mm above floor**.
- **Stairs (2 off — entrance & kitchen):** rise **170 mm**, run **230 mm**. Treads **47 × 250** rest both ends on ledgers **50 × 50 × 180** fixed to stringers **60 × 219**. Stringers lean against the floor joist at the top and fix to the concrete landing with **angle-iron plates 63 × 63 × 200** at the bottom (fig. 33).

### Ceiling
- **Noggings 38 × 50 @ 610 crs** fixed between roof trusses first (fig. 34). Ceiling of **6 mm plywood or 6 mm cement-bonded particleboard** (Table 1 lists 4 mm MR plywood 2' × 8'), nailed to noggings and trussed rafters **in a zigzag pattern**. Ceiling in the **main building only**, not the kitchen.

### Services
- Plumbing/electrical/sewerage can start as soon as roofing sheets are on.
- **Wiring holes:** 25 mm diameter drilled at the **centre of the section** of wall frames and floor joists; ≥ **300 mm from ends** of studs/joists and from any other hole (fig. 35: electric socket fixed to 50 × 50 blocking).
- **Notches in joists** allowed only if: (1) in the **top edge**; (2) located **1/10–1/5 of the span from the support**; (3) depth ≤ **21 mm for a 145 mm joist**. Otherwise drill holes for piping (fig. 36: holes 300–550 mm from support, centred with 50/25/50 spacing on a 145 joist).
- Fixtures fix to the panel framing timbers; add extra noggings for heavy fixtures (wash basins, boilers).

### Finishing touches
- **Plywood skirtings 9 × 100 mm** between wall and floor (main building); **plywood beadings 9 × 72 mm** cover all plywood joints.
- Paint the whole building (protection, cleaning, appearance — unfinished exposed wood weathers quickly). Floors: natural colour/grain attractive; apply floor finish against wear/abrasion. Building ready for occupation.

## Chapter 7 — Maintenance and repair

Two enemies of the tropical timber house: **termite attack** and **moisture/damp decay**.

**Raised platform:** keep underfloor space well ventilated (no blocking by grasses/shrubs/stored items); no standing water under the building — soil grades away from the house; keep tops of all concrete (footings, stair landing, kitchen floor) **50–100 mm above ground** as designed; inspect regularly for termite activity — **break earthen termite tunnels** (subterranean termites need soil-to-building contact); treat soil with insecticide if possible.

**Exterior walls:** no part should collect water (moisture → fungi, decay — common on door/window frames); seal shrinkage/workmanship cracks with putty; rust spots at exposed non-corrosive nail heads — set heads below surface and putty over; repaint deteriorated finishes.

**Interior:** fill cracks with putty and paint; don't let moisture linger on timber/plywood (especially kitchen); sheathing damage — small holes puttied and sanded, larger holes cut out and a new section fitted; floor-board shrinkage gaps (boards laid at high MC) — glue matching timber strips into gaps, or replace floor sections in severe cases.

**Roof:** ceiling stains indicate leaks — locate and repair; maintain roof-space air circulation — keep gable-end ventilation openings provided and unblocked.
