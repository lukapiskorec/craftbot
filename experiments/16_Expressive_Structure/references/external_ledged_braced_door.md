# External source: ledged-and-braced board door

Searched online with the Designer's approval on 2026-09-14, because no manual of the 17 in manuals/INDEX.md (including both woodworking volumes) names or draws this door type. Not a manual rule; do not present as one.

## Sources

- Ledge and Brace Doors: Timeless Joinery for British Homes, Arborfield Joinery, https://www.arborfieldjoinery.com/blogs/news/ledge-and-brace-doors-timeless-joinery-for-british-homes, accessed 2026-09-14. States unframed door thickness 20-30 mm (framed variant about 44 mm), and "the brace must point upwards from the hinge side" toward the latch side, so the timber's weight transfers back to the hinges and the wall.
- Types of Doors Used in Building Construction, theconstructor.org, https://theconstructor.org/building/types-of-doors/11898/, accessed 2026-09-14. States battens (vertical boards) 100-150 mm wide by 20-30 mm thick; ledges (horizontal rails) 100-200 mm wide by 25-30 mm thick; braces 100-150 mm wide by 25-30 mm thick; braces rise from the hinging side so they act as struts in compression.
- Making a framed ledge and brace door, UK DIY Wiki, https://wiki.diyfaq.org.uk/index.php/Making_a_framed_ledge_and_brace_door, accessed 2026-09-14. States "the braces should rise up from the hinge side to the free side of the door," placing them in compression and preventing sag; no numeric dimensions given.
- Construction of Ledge Doors, Lost Art Press (reproducing Doormaking and Window-making, a period carpentry text), https://blog.lostartpress.com/2020/08/04/construction-of-ledge-doors/, accessed 2026-09-14. States "the brace should always be at the bottom of the door on the hanging side, so that it is in compression, not in tension"; no numeric dimensions in the excerpt fetched.

## What the three independent sources agree on

Brace direction (unanimous across all four pages, the one number this search was for): each brace runs diagonally from the bottom corner on the hinge side up to the top corner on the latch side, so the brace works in compression and carries the door's weight back to the hinges rather than sagging at the latch corner.

Typical named ranges (theconstructor.org only; the other three pages give no numbers): battens 100-150 mm wide x 20-30 mm thick; ledges 100-200 mm wide x 25-30 mm thick; braces 100-150 mm wide x 25-30 mm thick. The concept's B board (24x120 mm) and F ledge/brace (30x120 mm) both sit inside these named ranges.

## Not verified

No manual or the pages found here give a nailing schedule for board-to-ledge or brace-to-ledge fixing, or a rule for how far the ledges sit from the door's top and bottom edges. Not carried into the table.
