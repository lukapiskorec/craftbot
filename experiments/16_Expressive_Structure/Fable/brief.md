# Brief as understood

Build one small room, a single-variation run. The floor plan is square and the building stands twice as tall as it is wide, the proportion measured on the outside of the finished structure. One entrance, one door. Daylight enters only from high up, either through the roof or through a band of windows just under it; the Designer picks one of the two from the red building photos and records the choice. Every member is a thin timber slat: walls, floor, roof and the outer trusses alike are built up from slats, with no sawn heavy sections, no sheet material and no glass modelled beyond the openings. The three photos of the red timber building in `input/` (01-soane-front, 02-soane-outside, 03-soaneinterior-1) set the construction method, the slat sizes and the overall dimensions. The two other images (Erne_Dachbau_778, Tchumi-02) set only the articulation of the roof and the outer structure: a series of trusses whose outlines undulate from one to the next, so that the outside reads as an expressive, rippling structure while the room inside stays a plain square box. Nothing from the red building is preserved literally; its proportions are dropped where they conflict with the 2:1 rule, and the brief wins over the photos on that point.

## Materials overridden

- The red building photos give construction and dimensions, but the 2:1 height rule of the brief overrides whatever proportion the photos show. Recorded as a deliberate deviation in the rationale.
- The Erne Dachbau and Tschumi images give articulation only. Their dimensions, materials and programme are ignored.

## Ambiguities resolved

- "Twice as tall as it is wide": the overall height of the outer structure, ridge or truss top to ground, is twice the outer plan width. The Designer decides how much of that height is the interior room and how much is roof structure; the room inside should still read as a tall square shaft, not a low box under a tall hat.
- "Light from either the roof or the windows close to the roof": one of the two, not both. The Designer chooses whichever the red building photos support and the truss roof allows, and records the reason.
- "Entirely out of thin timber slats": the trusses are built-up slat trusses (laminated, nailed or bolted slat chords and webs), not solid sawn trusses. Slat sizes come from the photos, checked against the manuals by the Researcher.
- "Undulating trusses": the trusses are a series along the building, each with a curved or folded outline, and the outline changes from truss to truss so that the outer surface ripples. The Designer sets the number of trusses, the amplitude and the rule that governs the change.
- Site: flat ground, no terrain, no context buildings.

## Scope decisions at the concept check (2026-09-14, CraftBot)

- The row of 11 ring trusses is 6.62 m long while the box is 4.36 m long, so two trusses at each end stand beyond the end walls and the door is reached through two open truss bays. Accepted: it is the only way the outer structure can be square in plan with a directional series of trusses, and the brief measures the 2:1 rule on the outer structure. The bays carry no roof.
- One concrete raft, 7.20 x 7.20 x 0.25 m with its top at ground, is the single non-timber element. Accepted as the modelled ground; the brief's "entirely out of thin timber slats" describes the building, not the ground it stands on.
- The room inside comes out at about 2.9:1 (3.90 m clear, about 11.4 m to the roof trusses). Accepted: the brief puts 2:1 on the outside, and asks the inside to read as a tall shaft.
- Piece count: the concept estimates about 3200 pieces, 1780 of them in the ring trusses. The Builder may apply the concept's own fallback (truss panel length 0.80 to 1.00 m, truss count and module unchanged) if one render-and-check pass takes longer than 15 minutes, and must record the switch in `version_notes.md`. No other reduction without a CraftBot decision.
