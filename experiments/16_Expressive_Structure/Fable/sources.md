# Researcher sources, experiment 16 (Fable)

Question set from the Designer's spawn message, answered from concept.md sections 3.2, 3.3, 3.5, 3.6 only. Manuals consulted, in order: index descriptions, chapter lists, extracted .md in full, then exact PDF pages named by each .md figure index. Manuals used: Canadian Wood-Frame House Construction (CMHC), Construction Manual of Prefabricated Timber House (FRIM), Introduction to timber frame construction (TRADA WIS 0-3), The Segal method, Advanced Framing Construction Guide (APA), Architect's Handbook of Construction Detailing. Manuals opened at index/chapter level and set aside as not on point: How to CLT and both CLT Handbooks, Canadian CLT Handbook, Binderholz, Stora Enso, Mass Timber Design Manual (all mass-timber panel systems, no thin-slat framing), London Housing Design Guide (space standards, not framing), Merronbrook pocket guide (erection tolerances, no member sizes), both Woodworking volumes (furniture joinery; searched by name for a ledged-and-braced door, no hit).

Halving/lapping at an X crossing: not shown in any manual (the concept already cites the timber-framing skill for this, outside manual scope). Horizontal ties: FRIM's horizontal brace, 22x97 mm, "at right angles to trusses" (Table 1 item E2, Ch.6 p.41) is the closest analogue, tying trussed frames together partway up; Segal Method's tie beam (p.9-10, Fig.44) ties frame bases, a different function (removes base thrust, not a mid-height tie). ## 3. Not covered

- Exact F 30x120 / B 24x120 sections. No manual states these metric sizes; the closest structural precedent (FRIM's 35x72 mm truss members, 47x72/47x97 mm studs) is thicker. The concept's pairing strategy (two or more thin slats face to face) is the model's own compensating device, not a sourced number. Recommendation: keep as designed; record as a labelled departure from manual member sizes, justified by repetition per 3.2.
- Paired-chord/single-web sandwich truss connection at the nodes. No manual shows this exact assembly. Recommendation (extension by analogy): nail or bolt through both chord slats and the web at every node, following CMHC's built-up-beam nailing rule (89 mm nails from each side, not more than 450 mm apart) and FRIM's truss-gusset nail spacing (46 mm along grain, 23 mm across grain, 16 mm edge distance) as the nearest sourced nailing density, scaled to the smaller F section.
- Continuous clerestory/window band framing. No manual draws this. Recommendation: extend the ordinary framing-around-openings rule (CMHC Fig.68; APA p.15-16) by letting the regular-spacing studs continue past the sill line as mullions with no added members, which is what the concept already does.
- Stud/mullion height beyond about 4.2 m. CMHC's Table 25 tops out at 4.2 m; nothing in any manual reaches the concept's 11.53 m stud run. The concept's own restraint interval (a rail every 0.65 m) substitutes for a manual span table; not itself contradicted by any source, but not validated by one either.
- Header bearing length on a jack stud. No manual states this for a stud-wall lintel; CMHC's 89 mm beam-on-masonry bearing (p.94) is a different condition (a beam on concrete, not a header on a timber jack stud) and is not carried into the model as a number.
- Joist/sleeper spacing specific to a slab-bearing floor. CMHC's Fig.47B shows the sleeper-on-slab assembly but no manual gives a spacing number for it; the elevated-joist spacing/thickness numbers (Table 22) are used by analogy in the rule table above.

## 4. Contradictions

- CMHC (p.109, quick reference) and APA (p.15-16) do not contradict each other: both set the jack-stud-doubling/omission threshold well above the concept's 1.0 m door opening (3 m and 1.22 m respectively), and both are more permissive than the concept's own doubled-jack choice in 3.3.8. Read together as agreeing that the concept is conservative, not as a conflict to resolve.
- FRIM's diagonal roof brace angle (45 degrees, Ch.6 p.41) and CMHC's roof strut angle ("not less than 45 degrees," Fig.86, p.125) agree exactly; no contradiction, used as converging support for the 45-degree figure applied by analogy in Q3 and Q7.
- No other contradictions found between the sources consulted for this question set; where a source was silent, it is recorded under "Not covered" rather than forced into agreement or disagreement with another source.

## Not asked, noticed

- CMHC's raised-heel truss rule (heel depth at least 250 mm at the exterior wall plane, p.56, for insulation clearance) does not apply to the concept's uninsulated box roof and was not carried into the table above.
- FRIM's joist notching rule (top edge only, between one-tenth and one-fifth of the span from the support, depth up to 21 mm for a 145 mm joist) and CMHC's equivalent (notch depth not more than one-third joist depth, never on the bottom) were noticed while reading Ch.6/Ch.19 but the concept sections read do not raise a notching question.
