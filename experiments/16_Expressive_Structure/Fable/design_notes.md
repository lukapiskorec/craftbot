# Design notes, experiment 16, Fable run

Decisions in the order I made them, with the alternatives that lost. Applies to v01 unless a later entry says otherwise. Dated 2026-09-14.

## Concept stage

**Roles of the images.** The three red-building photos give construction and proportion (photo rules 1 to 8 in concept.md); Erne gives the truss type, Tschumi the surface behaviour (rules 9, 10). I measured nothing on the last two.

**Calibration.** The only object of known size in photo 01 is the external stair: 14 risers, about 2.4 m, over 255 px, so about 105 px/m. I cross-checked with the board count on the far wall in photo 03 (31 boards at 130 to 140 mm, a 4.1 to 4.3 m room). The door was no use for calibration: it reads as a squat hatch about 0.8 m wide and 0.75 m tall, so either the room is 4 m wide and the door is tiny, or the door is normal and the room is 8 m wide and 17 m tall. The board count settles it for the small room; I read the door as a deliberate oddity of the original and replaced it (see below).

**Light: the band, not the roof.** Photo 01 shows a translucent corrugated band 1.8 m tall under an opaque roof; photo 03 shows the white band at the top of the side walls and a dark deck. Both photos say band. A rooflight would also sit under the ring trusses' top chords and be shaded by them. The band is open framing, no glass, per the brief. Rejected: a rooflight (unsupported by the photos, shaded by the trusses); band plus rooflight (brief says one of the two).

**Undercroft dropped.** The red building stands on a 2.4 m undercroft of heavy sawn posts with an external stair. The brief forbids heavy sections, puts the building on flat ground and wants one entrance. So the room stands on a raft flush with the ground, floor 0.144 above it. Rejected: a slat-framed undercroft (adds a storey the brief did not ask for and turns the one entrance into a stair).

**Two systems, not one.** The box carries itself; the ring trusses stand 0.08 m outside it and brace it. I tried the alternative first, the trusses carrying the box roof on their inner top chords, and it fails geometrically: the webs rise from the inner chord through any deck laid on it, in every sandwich arrangement I drew (webs between the chord slats, webs on the chord faces, purlins between nodes). Only a deck hung under the inner chord avoids the collision, and then the "roof" is a ceiling with no bearing for a covering. The red building has the same two-system logic (photo rule 2), so the separation is also the more faithful reading. Consequence: the box has its own slat roof trusses, which photo 03 shows anyway.

**Module 0.65 m and 11 trusses.** The plan is square inside (brief) and the outer structure should be square too, otherwise "twice as tall as wide" has two answers. Ring trusses wrap the box in one direction only (Erne, one direction), so the row's length in y has to match the trusses' width in x, about 6.6 m. Ten bays of 0.65 give 6.5 m between end planes, and 6 x 0.65 = 3.90 gives the room, so studs, floor joists, roof trusses and ring trusses share one module and one origin. Rejected: 0.60 module with 11 trusses (row 6.1 m, not square); 0.60 module with 12 trusses (no truss at y = 0, room 4.2, outer 6.9 x 6.7, height 13.8); 9 trusses at 0.80 (the ripple reads as steps, adjacent trusses differ by up to 0.2 m over 0.8 m).

**Ripple rule.** d(s, i) = 0.63 + 0.30 sin(2 pi s / 8.0 + 2 pi i / 11), s the arc length along the inner outline, i the truss index. Depth 0.33 to 0.93 m: the minimum keeps webs steeper than 40 degrees on a 0.8 m panel, the maximum is what fits a 6.62 m outer width. Wavelength 8.0 m: 1.5 waves per leg, half a wave over the top, an arc like Erne's rather than a serration. Phase 1/11 turn per truss: adjacent trusses differ by at most 0.17 m, and the crest line crosses the side elevation diagonally, which is the Tschumi effect. Rejected: a ripple in the inner chord too (the inner chord is what the box ties to and what stands on the shoe; it should be straight); a wave symmetric about the apex (kills the diagonal travel); amplitude 0.20 (a 0.4 m ripple on a 6.6 m elevation is 6 percent, hard to see).

**Inner outline as a 1:6 gable.** The ring's inner top chord follows the box roof at 0.07 clear, so the box roof and the ring have the same pitch. A flat inner chord would need a flat box roof with no fall. 1:6 is below the 1:3 threshold where ceiling ties stop being required, but the box roof trusses tie their heels anyway.

**Truss plane 120 mm thick, webs in two planes.** With one web plane (chord + 30 gap + chord) the two webs meeting at a node collide. A 60 mm gap gives the rising webs and the falling webs their own 30 mm plane, each nailed to the chord slat on its side. Rejected: gusseted nodes (no sheet material); lapping the webs (an extra joint type per node).

**Y-bracing in the gap.** The braces between ring legs cannot lie on the outer chord (it ripples) or inside the truss depth (webs). They lie flat on the legs' inner faces, in the 0.082 m gap between the box and the legs, as two X's per side with halving joints at the crossings and horizontal ties at the X ends. The red building's red diagonals sit in the same place relative to its box. Rejected: single tension diagonals per side (a compression diagonal 8.7 m long in a slat buckles, and the two sides would have to share load through the box); braces inside the truss depth (collide with webs).

**Door 1.00 x 2.10 with doubled jacks.** The module gives 1.12 clear between single jacks on the +/-0.65 kings; doubled jacks bring it to 1.00. Opening size follows the door, not the grid. Header of four slats on edge (120 x 120) for a 1.0 m span under a light roof. Rejected: the photo's squat 0.8 x 0.75 hatch (not a door); a header of paired slats only (60 wide, would not fill the stud zone and the cripples would land on nothing).

**Raft.** One concrete element, 7.20 x 7.20 x 0.25, top at the ground. It is the modelled ground, not a timber member. Rejected: pads under each truss foot plus a slab under the box (22 pads plus a slab, more elements for the same statement); no foundation (truss feet on nothing).

**Piece count.** About 3200 pieces, 1780 of them in the ring trusses. If the check loop cannot carry it, the panel length goes from 0.80 to 1.00 first; the truss count and module stay. Reported to CraftBot.

## Research round (v01)

**What the manuals settled.** Board spans (CMHC Tables 22 and 35, FRIM A7): both boards are conservative at 0.65 m. Heel bearing over the wall plate (CMHC Fig. 86). 1:6 is CMHC's low-slope threshold, so the box roof would need a membrane; not modelled by the brief's rule against sheet material. A 1.0 m door needs one jack per side; my doubled jacks are for the opening size, kept. Post base as a steel U-strap with two bolts (FRIM Sheet 7), named. Sleeper on slab over polyethylene (CMHC Fig. 47 B), named.

**What the manuals corrected.** Brace angle: CMHC and FRIM both want 45 degrees or steeper; my two X's per side across the whole 6.5 m row stood at 42 degrees. Changed to four X's per side in 3.25 m wide panels, 61 degrees, 16 diagonals in all. Rejected: three X's over the height across the full row (steeper is taller and narrower, so more panels over the height make it worse, 31 degrees); a single K per panel (a compression diagonal in a 30 mm slat over 6 m).

**What the manuals do not reach, and what I did instead.** No manual states 30 x 120 or 24 x 120 as structural members (FRIM's thinnest are 35 x 72 truss members); no stud table goes past 4.2 m (CMHC Table 25) against my 11.5 m; CMHC's minimum column is 140 x 140. The brief asks for thin slats, so these are departures by instruction, and the compensating devices are the ones in the concept: pairing, rails every 0.65 m on both faces, ties to the ring legs at every rail, and the trusses rather than the studs taking the wind. TRADA's 600 mm batten maximum against my 0.65 m rails: 50 mm over, kept for the one-module argument. The sandwich truss node and the clerestory band are extensions by analogy and are labelled so in concept 4. All of it goes into the rationale's deviation ledger under "outside the manuals".

**External search.** Approved one: a ledged-and-braced board door, because no manual names it. The leaf is a small element; the sizes in the concept are mine until the row lands and stay mine if the source disagrees only in millimetres.


## v01 Builder questions (2026-09-14, applies to v02 and later)

The v01 Builder found five places where a concept number contradicted the concept's own geometry and resolved each toward the checkable requirement, plus three open questions. My answers, with what lost. Concept numbers patched in place (sections 1, 3.2, 3.3.5, 3.3.6, 3.3.8, 3.5, 3.6), each patch marked "v01 derived constant" or "v02".

**1. Box roof end trusses at y = +/-1.89, confirmed.** The end wall's inner rail starts at 1.974 and its stud zone at 2.004; a 120 mm truss centred on 1.95 reaches 2.01 and cuts both. At 1.89 the outer slat face sits on the inner board plane. The truss still bears on the side wall head plate, which spreads the 60 mm offset from the stud pair at 1.95; the vertical-alignment rule is about load reaching a stud line, and a 60 mm eccentricity on a 120 mm plate over a 60 mm stud pair is within the plate. Lost: five trusses with the gable plate carrying the deck edge (deck span 0.76 at the ends breaks R-24's 0.65); moving the end walls out (changes the room, R-01).

**2. Deck eave at x = 2.249 and top chord tails at 2.22, confirmed.** My 2.28 was an arithmetic slip against my own leg inner face at 2.26. Nineteen whole 120 boards along a 1:6 slope give 2.249 in plan, no ripped board, 0.071 overhang past the wall face. The chord tails at 2.22 stay under the deck and clear the brace plane. Lost: legs moved out to 2.35 (changes W_out, every leg and the 2:1 tuning, for a 30 mm overhang).

**3. Brace plane at x = 2.230 to 2.260, confirmed.** The concept's sentence was right ("flat against the legs' inner faces") and its numbers wrong. The braces work only if they are nailed to every leg they cross, so contact is the rule and 2.198 to 2.228 was a typo. Clear of the wall face by 0.052; the box-to-leg ties are still named, not modelled.

**4. Upper tie at deck underside minus 10 mm (11.669 to 11.789), lower panel top at 6.07, confirmed.** The tie's job is to hold the top of the braced panel at the eave and tie the legs together there; 10 mm under the deck edge is as high as it gets without cutting the deck or the ring's corner mitre. Written as a rule, not a number, because z_h is tuned by the Builder. The 6.05 was another slip (20 mm overlap with the lower tie's top at 6.07). Lost: the deck pulled back to 2.22 to keep the tie at 11.75 (the deck would then overhang the wall by 42 mm and the tie would sit above the head plate for no reason).

**5. Splices, confirmed with one change.** The rule is now a number: splices on a rail centre (sill rail included) or a truss node, the two slats of a pair at least one module apart on studs and two panels apart on chords; "half a length" is gone. Side wall studs (A 4.55/9.10, B 2.60/7.15, stagger 1.95) and leg chords (A nodes 6/12, B nodes 4/10, stagger two panels) pass. The gable studs' third cut at the sill rail does not: it put the B splice at 9.57, 0.47 from the A splice at 9.10. B at 3.25/7.80 instead gives pieces 3.25, 4.55 and at most 4.37 m to the apex (deck underside 11.85 to 12.17), two splices per slat, stagger 1.30 from A, all on rails. Same set for the door cripples. Lost: the third cut (more pieces, less stagger); a B set at 3.90/8.45 (stagger 0.65, the minimum, when 1.30 is free).

**6. Outer chord corners mitred, confirmed; the depth floor stays at 0.33.** The offset-polygon vertex is the honest geometry of an offset polyline; the chamfer was a shortcut in my node rule that the wave exposed at eight of 22 eave corners. Raising the minimum depth to 0.50 (d = 0.75 +/- 0.25) would push the maximum to 1.00, W_out to about 6.76 and every tuned number with it, and would still leave a chamfered shoulder at the eave where the ring should read as a gable on legs. Structurally the corner vertex is a kink in the outer chord with no web to it, about 0.4 m from the adjacent outer vertices each way; the chord is a nailed sandwich pair and the kink is a mitre in both slats, so the deviation force at the kink goes into the two webs either side. Recorded as an unbraced kink, plausible at this scale, not calculated. Lost: a web to the corner vertex (no free web plane at a corner node).

**7. R-28 covers the y-bracing only.** CMHC Fig. 86 and FRIM Fig. 30 are about braces that stiffen a frame or a wall. The leaf braces stop a 1.0 x 2.1 board door from sagging on its hinges; their angle follows from three ledges at 0.80 spacing on a 1.00 leaf, and ledged-and-braced doors run at 30 to 40 degrees as a type (the external row). Ledge spacing unchanged. Lost: two ledges at 1.60 spacing to steepen the braces (the boards would span 1.6 m between fixings, and the leaf is not a wall).

**8. Eave slot open, part of the band (new R-34).** Photo rule 4 has the translucent band directly under the opaque roof, and inside a white band under a dark deck with the slat trusses standing in it (rule 5). That is what the open slot gives: the band runs from the sill rail at 9.63 to the deck underside, the head plate and head rails are a transom across it, and the roof truss heels stand in the band as in photo 03. A board strip between the heels would put a dark fascia between the band and the roof, which neither photo shows. Structurally nothing changes: the deck diaphragm reaches the side wall through the truss heels on the head plate, not through any board in the slot. R-05 reworded so the slot is not "another opening". Lost: a 0.24 board fascia between heels (unlike the photos, about 60 pieces for nothing the load path needs).

**Not asked, accepted as read.** Derived constants 6 to 8 of the v01 notes: the end walls drop the +/-1.95 module stud where it overlaps the end stud (R-15 reworded); the door leaf's hinge stile against the jamb lining at -2.004 so the open leaf touches the wall; the bottom plate and outer bottom rail continuous under the door as the one threshold step. The four-slat corner post above the side head plate and the 30 x 30 notch in the outer rail at each eave corner are Builder detailing inside the concept and need no line.

## Phase 2, structural review (2026-09-14, on v03, reference set aside)

Read from `version_notes.md` v01 to v03 and `inspection_v03.md`, not from the concept. The checklist of the structural-logic skill, item by item.

**1. Vertical load paths, as sentences.** Ring trusses: outer and inner chord pairs of every ring end on the raft at z = 0 at both feet (steel U-strap and two bolts named, FRIM Sheet 7, not modelled); webs between the chord slats carry the truss's own weight down both chords. Box roof: deck boards on the top chords of 7 sandwich trusses at 0.65 (end pair at +/-1.89), bottom chords on the side wall head plates over the full stud zone, head plates on paired studs at 0.65 (spliced one slat at a time on rail centres, partner continuous), studs on the bottom plate on the raft; the gable head plates carry the deck edge straight into the end wall studs. Door: header of four slats on doubled jacks on the plate. Floor: boards on joists lying on the raft. Braces and ties: nailed to the legs they cross. Every family closes to the raft; nothing floats (0 floating in v03).

**2. Lateral, per direction.** Across the trusses (x): 11 rings with trussed corners (the mitred outer vertex at each eave and the webs from the corner node to both neighbouring outer vertices make the corner a truss, so "portal" is earned, not assumed); the box is tied to the legs at rail levels (named). Each ring takes its own 0.65 m strip; no diaphragm needed. Along the row (y): the two braced planes at x = +/-2.245 (four X's per side at 59 to 61 degrees, ties at 6.0 and 11.6) from z 0.15 to 11.7; the box deck brings the end walls' wind to the side walls through the truss heels on the head plates, and the rails and ties bring it to the legs. The braced planes reach the eave. Above the upper tie the 11 ring tops (4.7 m arches, 1.4 m above the tie) are tied to nothing in y: each top run stands as a cantilever from its own braced legs. Finding, see 6.

**3. Bearing at seats, continuity.** Bottom chords on head plates for 0.12 (the stud zone); top chords seated on the bottom chord's arris at the heel; deck on chord tops across every truss; joists flat on the raft; truss feet square on the raft; header on jacks 0.12 each side; bottom plate and outer bottom rail continuous under the door (the threshold). Ring chord splices on nodes, two panels apart in a pair; stud splices on rail centres one module apart. Discontinuities walked: door (header, cripples), eave slot (the deck reaches the side wall only through the seven heels, a named nailed connection, fine for a diaphragm on a 4.3 m span), gable corners (four-slat corner post from the side head plate to the gable plate).

**4. Foundations.** The raft is the modelled ground; undersides irrelevant on flat ground. FRIM's 50 mm footing upstand under each truss foot and the polyethylene under the joists are not modelled (no terrain, no moisture in the vocabulary). Overturning of a 13.2 m tall, 6.6 m wide frame is held by the named hold-downs at 44 feet and a 31 t raft; not calculated, recorded.

**5. Sizes and restraint, the one real finding.** The sections are outside every manual and are recorded (R-32). What the model does not yet do is give the ring chords a weak-axis restraint. The inner leg chords are crossed by the braces and ties every 2.5 to 3 m, which is a restraint. The outer chords, 28 m of paired 30 mm slats per ring, are restrained out of their plane by nothing at all between the raft and the raft: the top run's outer chord is a compression chord under its own weight and under wind on the row, the leg's outer chord is in compression under wind from the far side, and a 30 x 120 slat pair with a 28 m unbraced length is a mechanism, not a member. The inner top chords over the roof are in the same state (no tie in y except through the legs). In a built Erne roof the purlins and the roof plane do this job; here the ripple forbids a straight purlin, but short spacers between adjacent trusses do not need to be straight across the row. Action: requirement P2-01, spacers F 30 x 120 between the chord faces of adjacent rings, at every third outer vertex along the outer chord (about every 2.4 m along the outline; each spacer runs between the two outer vertices at the same s, so it is inclined by the depth difference, at most 0.17 over 0.53) and at every inner node of the top runs (six per bay), nailed to both chord faces. About 120 outer and 60 inner pieces. Rejected: straight purlins over the outer chords (cannot lie on a rippling surface); nothing (the weak-axis length stays 28 m; the review exists to catch exactly this).

**6. Lateral in y above the tie.** With the spacers the 11 tops become a grid, and a grid without diagonals holds only by chord bending. Action: P2-02, one zigzag of diagonals per slope in the roof plane, F 30 x 120 laid flat in the 0.07 gap under the inner top chords (top face on the chord undersides, 0.04 clear of the deck), from the eave line to the apex line over three bays each (plan angle about 50 degrees; FRIM Sheet 9 puts its roof diagonal at 45), from truss 0 to truss 9 and its mirror so they form X's; bay 9 to 10 stays unbraced, the end truss being held by the spacers. 16 pieces of about 3.05 m. Rejected: diagonals on top of the outer chords (the ripple); diagonals under the deck inside the box (R-08, the inside stays plain).

**7. Details by gravity, sliding, moisture, racking.** Posts on the slab: shoes named, upstand not modelled. Roof drains at 1:6 to both eaves over the open eave slot and past the 0.08 gap; no gutter in the vocabulary, recorded. Halving joints thin each brace to 15 mm at the crossing; standard for slat X-bracing, kept. Racking of the box: two board faces on rails; kept.

**Considered and not changed.** The box roof trusses' 0.12 heel depth for a 4.25 m span (apex depth about 0.5, span/9, plausible for a light deck); the 0.24 m open eave slot (the diaphragm goes through the heels, not through boards); the porch bays' free trusses (braced by the same planes as the rest); the 60 x 120 studs at 11.3 m (restrained every 0.65 on both faces, and the wind is the rings' job, not theirs).

Closing line: every section is plausible for its span, none is calculated.

## v04 Builder questions 10 to 12 (2026-09-14, applies to v04 as built; no v05)

**10. Inner spacers: 7 per bay, both eave corners in, confirmed as built.** The review clause said 6 and the text said "every inner node of both slopes"; the text is the rule, the 6 was me counting two runs of three nodes and forgetting that the two runs share no node but each starts at an eave corner. The load path wants the corners: the roof-plane zigzags of P2-02 start at the eave line, and a bracing panel needs a strut across the bay at its eave chord as much as at the apex, or the X's end on a chord that can move in y between trusses. The upper tie 0.16 lower holds the leg on its inner face, not the top chord's start node. Lost: 5 per bay (no corners; the X's eave ends unstrutted, and the corner is the one node where the outer chord's mitre and the inner chord's corner meet, so it is the node that most wants holding); the literal asymmetric 6 (a counting slip, not a design). Outer spacers 13 per bay counted from the apex, none on the raft, as the Builder chose; 200 spacers in all. P2-01's check clause now says 13 + 7.

**11. Roof-plane diagonals: 12, confirmed as built.** Three-bay panels 0-3, 3-6, 6-9 give three diagonals per zigzag, two zigzags per slope, two slopes: 12, each three boxes at the halving joints, 36 pieces. My "16" in review item 6 was arithmetic, not a fourth panel. Lost: 2.25-bay panels for four per zigzag (the panel ends would not land on truss planes, so the diagonals could not be clipped plumb at a truss and would meet the apex and eave lines between spacers); a 12-bay row (scope). P2-02 now says 12 diagonals, 36 pieces.

**12. z_h and the upper tie.** Concept 3.4 already carries z_h = z_e - 0.37 with 11.484 built in v03 and 3.5 carries the tie at 11.575 to 11.695 with the v01 11.669 to 11.789 kept as history, both patched by the previous Designer.
