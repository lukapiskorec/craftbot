# Inspection v02 — Level 3

## Summary

- The two-wing ring, deeper south link, one-bay north link, and open central court are visible in views 01–05; no off-site building mass is apparent.
- Five occupied facade bands and repeated exterior shafts/corbels are visible; Level 3 facade close-up confirms distinct beams, slabs, panels, and window modules (view 14).
- Four curved stair positions and open wells are visible in view 23; the close-up (view 27) shows connected-looking flights, finite landings, and dark railings.
- No floating or visibly unsupported stair/member is apparent in views 18 and 27, but bearing, header continuity, 0.15 m separation, 2.10 m headroom, and 1.20 m route widths are not visually proven.
- R17/R18 interior organization and continuous upper circulation cannot be confirmed from the assigned views; the facade/section views do not expose enough partitions and links.
- R21 is visually confirmed by views 18 and 27: exposed frame, stair void, and daylight/open gallery read clearly.
- R22 and R24 remain builder checks; the views cannot establish foundation continuity or every slab-opening edge support.

## Per-view findings

- **Views 01–04:** Elevated orbits show a continuous rectangular ring around an open-to-sky courtyard, with the partial northern insert and its rooflight strip; the court is not roofed over. The south end is visibly deeper than the north end, and the building sits within the site planes.
- **View 05:** Top view confirms the long thin two-wing topology, a deeper south link, a shorter north link, the northern partial insert, and a narrow east-side passage beside it. Four stair openings are not exposed in this roof-level top view.
- **View 16:** Long elevation shows five occupied horizontal bands, projecting exterior columns, repeated corbel shoulders, concrete spandrels, and broad glazed units with narrow vertical lights. The render is monochrome, so material contrast and transparent-glass behavior are only partly judgeable.
- **View 14:** Close facade view confirms repeated horizontal precast members, regular panel joints, exterior shafts outside the glazing line, and enlarged/chamfered corbel shoulders at each level. No obvious panel gap or floating corbel is visible.
- **View 18:** Long section shows the curved stair stack through the occupied levels, open central voids, landings at each floor, and exposed beam/slab frame. The upper flights and landings appear to connect to floor edges, but exact headroom and route widths cannot be measured here.
- **View 23:** Top cutaway shows four separate curved stair locations around the building, with open circular wells and no enclosed-cylinder substitute. The insert rooflight grid remains visible in the north court.
- **View 27:** Stair close-up shows fine dark railings, curved half-turn flights, finite landings, open wells, and exposed beams/corbels. The view does not prove the required 22-riser count, 230-degree advance, rail duplication absence, or load-path connection into headers/rim.

## Previous findings status

- v01 reported a roofed courtyard and an orphan top landing; the v02 assigned views show the courtyard open and the stair system distributed around the building, so those visual findings appear fixed. Numeric/structural closure is outside this image-only pass.

## Requirements confirmed and rejected

- **Confirmed by eye:** R03, R06, R07, R08, R15 (four curved stair locations/open wells visually present), R16 (open stairs and fine railings), R21, R27 (assigned views include the required topology/facade/stair evidence), and the visual portions of R28.
- **Not confirmed:** R17/R18 (interior room asymmetry and continuous upper circulation), R22 (continuous support and stair bearing), and R24 (all slab discontinuity edge supports). R15’s numerical riser, clearance, headroom, and route-width clauses also remain unverified by these views.
